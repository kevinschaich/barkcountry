# Frequently Asked Questions

| ## Visitor Services<br>**Where can I find information or a ranger when I arrive?**<br>Southwest Park Entrance: [Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/kyvc.htm) is open year-round. Restrooms, water, and public WiFi are available 24/7 in the visitor center vestibule/lobby.<br>Northwest Park Entrance: Informational boards with area maps are located in the Loomis Plaza. The [Loomis Museum](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/loomismuseum.htm) is open seasonally and operates as a visitor contact station / information center.<br>**Are there showers?**<br>Coin operated 24-hour showers are located at the Manzanita Lake Camper Store during the summer season.<br>**Where can I stay without camping in the park?**<br>Drakesbad Guest Ranch is open June to October and is located in Warner Valley. Often, reservations need to be made at least 6 months in advance. For more information, please see the [Drakesbad Guest Ranch](http://drakesbad.com/) website. Rustic camping cabins are also available at Manzanita Lake. Please visit [lassenlodging.com](http://lassenlodging.com/) for information. For reservations, please call 877-444-6777 or visit [www.recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/).<br>**How much does it cost to visit the park?**<br>Park entrance fees apply year-round. A 7-day pass is $30 per vehicle in the high season or a Lassen Volcanic annual pass is $55. Lassen also honors Whiskeytown, Crater Lake, Lava Beds and Oregon cave passes. To learn more about park fees and passes, please visit [fees and passes.](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/fees.htm)<br>**Do I need a reservation to enter the park?**<br>No, at this time there is no reservation required to enter the park. However, we highly recommend making a reservation for a campground.<br>**Where can I purchase a pass for the park or federal lands?**<br>Passes can be purchased at the park's main entrance stations or [online](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/fees.htm). Be advised that October through most of May the entrances are only open on most Saturdays and Sundays, but a pass to visit the park is still required. To learn more about passes, please visit the [fees and passes](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/fees.htm) page.<br>**What are the park hours?**<br>The park is open 24 hours a day, year-round. Vehicle access in winter and spring is limited due to snow accumulation and closed park roads, but visitors can still drive to either main park entrance and enjoy snow activities such as snowshoeing or skiing.<br>**Is Lassen National Park Highway Open?**<br>The opening and closing dates of the park highway vary from year to year depending on the total accumulation of snow. The road can close as early October or as late as December. The road can open as early as May or as late as July. Visit the [current conditions](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) or [spring road opening](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/winter-road-closures-and-spring-clearing-update.htm) webpage for up-to-date information.<br>**When did the Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center open?**<br>The visitor center opened September 21, 2008. The center offers interactive exhibits, a 20-minute park movie, concession cafe and gift shop, rangers to help with your visit, and a non-profit association book store with educational merchandise. The center is a platinum rated LEED certified building.<br>**What happened to the old Chalet?**<br>The chalet was a dilapidated building which was deconstructed in June 2005 to make way for the new visitor center. Furthermore, the chalet was not a visitor center and minimally served the public in the summer season only. It was originally constructed to host skiers through the winter season in the early 1960s. Much of the building was recycled including beams and concrete.<br>**Where can I find a bite to eat?**<br>Food service is provided at the Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center and at the Manzanita Lake Camper Store. Food service is seasonal. Please visit [here](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/hours.htm) for specific hours and seasons.<br>**Are there shuttles provided?**<br>The park nor any provate operators provide a shuttle service.<br>**Does the park have WiFi?**<br>WiFi is available in the Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center and at the Loomis Plaza at Manaznita Lake.<br>**Can I get cell phone coverage in the park?**<br>AT&T and Verizon provide the most extensive coverage. Areas along the road such as Bumpass Hell and Lassen Peak parking areas, and the Chaos Jumbles turn-out. A few other turnouts on the southern side of the park offer reception. Developed campgrounds have no reception.<br>**Are there pay phones in the park?**<br>No, there are no pay phones in the park. The nearest payphone from the south entrance is located at the Mineral Lodge in Mineral.<br>**Where are restrooms located?**<br>Restrooms are at the Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center and the Loomis Plaza. There are several vault toilets in parking areas and campgrounds along the 30-mile park road. <br>## Camping<br>**Can I reserve a campsite at a specific campground?**<br>All campsites within the park available for reservations through the [www.recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/) website. Please visit the park [campground page](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/camping-in-campgrounds.htm) for a listing of campgrounds and information about each campground.<br>**Will I be able to find a site without a reservation?**<br>Reservations are required at all of Lassen's campgrounds. The best way to ensure you have a campsite when you arrive is to make a reservation at [recreation.gov.](https://www.recreation.gov/camping/gateways/2803)<br>**What campground is recommended?**<br>Lassen has seven campgrounds ranging from primitive and remote to developed and easier accessed. The smallest at 17 sites to the largest at 179 sites. All campsites have picnic tables, fire rings, and bear-proof storage lockers. Visit the [campground page](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/camping-in-campgrounds.htm) for more detailed information.<br>**How far is Manzanita Lake from Manzanita Lake Campground?**<br>The closest loop to the lake is Loop A. Loop D is furthest from the lake. There is a short footpath that leads from Loop A to the lakeshore. The distance is under a quarter mile. All loops are connected by footpaths. A short drive from the campground to the picnic area and boat launch by the lake is also an option.<br>**Can I see Manzanita Lake from my campsite?**<br>No, there are no views of the lake from any site at the campground.<br>**Can I see Lassen Peak from my site at Manzanita Lake Campground?**<br>Yes, there are filtered views from some of the sites at the campground.<br>**How far is Summit Lake from Summit Lake North and South Campgrounds?**<br>The lake is a short walk from any of the sites at either campground.<br>**Can I see summit Lake from my campsite?**<br>About one third of the campsites situated closest to the lake offer views.<br>**Are mosquitoes a problem?**<br>Mosquitoes are numerous as the snow melts and the marshy areas around Summit Lake fill with water in June and July. The mosquito activity reduces steadily as temperatures warm and water dries in August, September, and October.<br>**Is there shade at all of the campgrounds in the park?**<br>All of the campgrounds in the park are forested. Some sites within a campground have more shade than others, and some campgrounds have more shade than others. The Warner Valley Campground and Juniper Lake campground are within the burn area of the 2021 Dixie Fire. Some trees are still standing, but will provide less shade than the unburned trees at other campgrounds.<br>**What size are the bear boxes?**<br>3' wide x 3' long x 4' deep.<br>**Where can I have a fire?**<br>Campfires are allowed only in the fire rings provided in developed campgrounds. Fires are not allowed in the backcountry.<br>**Where can I get firewood?**<br>You are allowed to collect dead and downed wood or you may purchase firewood at the Manzanita Lake Camper Store or Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center for use in developed campground fire rings.<br>**How do I get a wilderness permit to camp in the backcountry?**<br>Wilderness permits must be obtained online through [recreation.gov.](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/4675334) Please look at the regulations and requirements for camping in the wilderness. There are no quota limits.<br>**Are bears a problem in the backcountry?**<br>Bears and other wildlife can be and have been known to seek out human food from backpackers and even day hikers. Be sure to properly store food and scented items out of bears' reach in an [approved canister](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/wilderness-permit-information.htm). Canisters can be rented at the park. [Download the bear safety bulletin](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/upload/Bear-safety-bulletin.pdf).<br>**Are there RV hookups?**<br>No. There is a water filling station and a dump station at Manzanita Lake available for a $8 fee. The nearest RV area with hook-ups is Volcano Country in Mineral or KOA in Shingletown.<br>## Rules and Regulations<br>**Where can I fish in the park?**<br>Fishing is permitted within the park according to [California State regulations](http://www.dfg.ca.gov/). A California license is required for ages 16 and up. Special regulations apply to Manzanita Lake: within 150 feet of the Manzanita Creek inlet is closed to fishing, the rest of the lake is catch and release only; artificial lures and single, barb less hooks must be used. Manzanita Creek is closed above Manzanita Lake year-round. [Learn more.](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/fishing.htm)<br>**Where can I walk my pet in the park?**<br>Not on trails, but pets are allowed in developed areas (campgrounds, picnic areas, paved roads, and parking areas). Pets must be on a leash. [More information about pets in the park.](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/pets.htm)<br>**Can dogs be left unattended?**<br>It is not a good idea to leave your dog unattended at your campsite; dogs can get loose, bark, or be in distress causing other visitors concern. Dogs can be left in vehicles as long as it is safe to do so and with adequate water and food.<br>**Where can I ride my bike in the park?**<br>Biking is permitted on developed roads only. Bikes are not permitted on hiking trails.<br>**Is snowmobiling allowed in the park?**<br>Snowmobiling is strictly prohibited inside the boundaries of Lassen Volcanic National Park. There are numerous snowmobiling trails in the Lassen National Forest, surrounding the park. Please visit the [National Forest](https://www.fs.fed.us/r5/lassen/conditions/snowmobile_trails/) website for further information.<br>**Does federal law allow us to have a loaded firearm in the park and within our trailer or campsite?**<br>Park visitors are allowed to possess a concealed loaded firearm if they have a valid State of California concealed carry weapons permit (CCW). Loaded firearms carried openly are prohibited in locations where use of the firearm is prohibited. Use of a firearm in the park is prohibited, therefore an openly carried loaded firearm in the park is also prohibited. One exception to that rule is your campsite, which is a temporary place of residence. Openly carrying a loaded firearm outside of your campsite is strictly prohibited. Firearms are not permitted in any park buildings, including campground and other rest stations.<br>## Recreation and Activities<br>**I'm only going to be at the park for a few hours or a couple of days, what can I do?**<br>Lassen has several options no matter what your time schedule. See [summer itineraries](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/summer.htm#summer_itineraries) for suggestions.<br>**Is a specific trail open, free of snow, and accessible?**<br>The opening dates of trails in the park vary from year to year depending on snow accumulation and other winter storm damage. Trails near Manzanita Lake, Butte Lake, and Warner Valley tend to thaw out first. Please visit the [current trail conditions webpage](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/trail_conditions.htm).<br>**What hiking trails are recommended?**<br>Lassen has over 150 miles of hiking trails ranging from easy to strenuous. Most of the miles of trails are interconnecting loops located in Lassen wilderness. Many trailheads can be accessed easily by vehicle along the park highway where explorers can discover mountain meadows, coniferous forests, clear mountain streams, breathtaking views and vistas, bubbling hydrothermal areas, and even ascend a volcano. For a list of trails and their distances, visit the [hiking page](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/hiking_lassen_park.htm).<br>**If I can't hike with my pet in the park, where is a good place to hike with my dog nearby?**<br>Caribou Wilderness (E), Thousand Lakes Wilderness (N), Sacramento River Bend (W), Ishi Wilderness (SW), Spencer Meadows Trail, Mill Creek Trail (outside of the park off of Hwy. 36).<br>**When is Bumpass Hell Trail open?**<br>This trail is closed every winter, due to hazards under the snow. It typically opens in mid-July.<br>**When is the Lassen Peak Trail open?**<br>The trail is most-easily hiked when snow has melted, typically between June and early July.<br>**How far is the hike on Lassen Peak and how long does it take?**<br>The hike is 5 miles round trip and takes between 3 to 5 hours to complete. Vertical gain of 2,000 feet with a series of switchbacks. It is unlawful to cut switchbacks and permits rapid erosion and trampling of delicate alpine plants. The trail is very exposed with limited shade. Take adequate water for each person, food, wear sturdy shoes, sunscreen, and wear layered clothing.<br>**Do we need crampons to ascend Lassen Peak in winter and spring?**<br>Crampons, as well as other mountaineering equipment is essential for the ascent and decent of Lassen Peak and other areas. Visitors who wish to do so, must be experienced in mountaineering and backcountry travel in a snow covered mountain environment. The equipment needed is determined by the conditions on a day to day basis. Having all necessary equipment and knowledge is key to a safe and enjoyable experience.<br>**Where is the volcanic activity?**<br>The most extensive and famous hydrothermal area in the park is Bumpass Hell, a 16 acre bowl filled with mudpots, fumaroles, and boiling pools. The hike is 3 miles, round trip. Elevation change is minimal. Be advised that in most years, the trail is not accessible until mid-July due to snow and ice coverage. Visit [Bumpass Hell](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/exploring-the-hydrothermal-areas.htm) for further information on this and other hydrothermal areas in the park. You may also visit the [trail update](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/trail_conditions.htm) page for access to Bumpass Hell and other park trails.<br>**What can we do with children?**<br>Visit Bumpass Hell or Sulphur Works hydrothermal areas (please watch your children carefully). Become a Junior Ranger--materials can be collected at visitor centers. For further information, visit the [For Kids](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/learn/kidsyouth/index.htm) webpage.<br>**Can I bring a stroller or wheelchair on the trails?**<br>There are two trails which are wheelchair and stroller (including jogging strollers) accessible: the Devastated Area Interpretive Trail and Sulphur Works Hydrothermal Area. <br>**Where can I go swimming or soak in hot springs?**<br>All park waters are available for swimming except hydrothermal waters. These waters are too hot and acidic. Smaller lakes dry up at the end of summer. Some lakes have many reeds or snags. Most lake temperatures remain on the cool to cold side.<br>**Where are the caves?**<br>There are no caves in the park. Lava Beds National Monument, near the Oregon border, has many caves. Subway Cave, included in Lassen National Forest is located 10 miles outside of the northwest entrance near the town of Old Station. Download a [PDF](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/upload/Subway-Cave.pdf) or visit the [Lassen National Forest webpage](https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/lassen/recarea/?recid=11435) for more information on Subway Cave.<br>## Weather<br>**What is the weather like?**<br>Weather plays a dramatic role at Lassen Volcanic National Park. Winter, especially, shapes the landscape; snow generally begins to accumulate each year in October, doesn't melt in most places until July, and can remain in isolated areas year round. Summer weather is more predictable, with warm, dry days, blue skies, and cool nights. Visitors to Lassen Volcanic National Park should be prepared for any kind of weather, any time of the year. Please visit our [weather webpage](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/weather.htm) for more information about temperatures you can expect during each month of the year, as well as current weather forecasts.<br>**When does it start snowing?**<br>Snow can fall anytime of the year, however winter settles in by early November on average.<br>## Winter<br>**What are the snow and avalanche conditions in the park?**<br>Please check [here](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/conditions.htm) for current snow conditions in the park. A good site to check before venturing out is the [Mt. Shasta Avalanche Advisory.](http://www.shastaavalanche.org/) Mt. Shasta and Lassen have similar conditions. Be advised that there is always risk in venturing out into avalanche terrain, no matter what the avalanche warning is. Snow and avalanche conditions can change constantly. Terrain and other factors can create a mix that is ideal for dangerous avalanche conditions. Snow conditions can vary greatly from day to day and at varying elevations and aspects. Many factors play a role in snow condition; temperature, sun exposure, open or forested terrain and wind. The age of the snowpack and new snow accumulation and the type of snow also play a role in the condition.<br>**What can I see in winter when access in the park is limited?**<br>Bring snowshoes and skis to explore a quieter side of the park. On clear days, views of Lassen and other peaks can be seen. Take a 2 mile trek around Manzanita Lake where view of snow-covered volcanoes can be seen and opportunities to spot birds and other winter wildlife abound. Take a one mile trek up the snow-covered park road to the Sulphur Works Hydrothermal area, accessed from the southwest entrance of the park. The new Kohm Yah-mah-nee Visitor Center is open daily and is located at the southwest entrance.<br>**On average, how much snow does the park get over a typical winter?**<br>Typically, the park receives 400 to 500 inches in a snow season ( November through April). This is measured at the southwest entrance of the park at an elevation of 6700 feet. there have been years when the park received over 700 inches in a snow season!<br>**When does it stop snowing and how long does the snow stick around?**<br>Snow usually stops falling sometime in April. Snow lingers in the high elevations of the Park such as Brokeoff Mountain, Lassen Peak and Bumpass Hell until early July.<br>**Where can I snowshoe, ski or sled?**<br>Recommended trails can be found on the [Winter Activities](https://www.nps.gov/lavo/planyourvisit/winter_activities.htm) webpage. Trails at the south entrance of the park are classified as intermediate to advanced. Trails at the north entrance of the park are classified as beginner to intermediate. The park does not rent snowshoes or skis.<br>## Science & Nature<br>**Where is the volcanic activity?**<br>There are several locations where hydrothermal (hot water) features are in the park. The most popular and largest, Bumpass Hell, is located about 6 miles from the southwest entrance and includes a 3-mile round-trip hike. Another area is Sulphur Works, 1 mile from the southwest entrance. In Warner Valley, Boiling Springs Lake and Devils Kitchen offer an otherworldly experience.<br>**Where is the lava?**<br>All rock in the park is volcanic in origin. Visit Devastated Area Interpretive Trail for a short descriptive walk. Learn about Lassen's eruption and discover the variety of lava that is found in the park. <br>**What is "Cold Boiling Lake"?**<br>Just like the name implies, a portion of the cold water lake appears to be boiling. This illusion is caused by carbon dioxide gasses escaping through the water to its surface, giving the appearance of boiling.<br>**What is the red stuff on the snow?**<br>Snow algae also known as watermelon algae.<br>**What causes some of the branches on the trees to die?**<br>Also known as "branch flagging," this phenomenon is caused by a fungal canker disease and dwarf mistletoe. The two pathogens work together. The flagging is more pronounced during the dry season and is common among the Red Fir.<br>**What is the large leafed plant with the yellow flower along the slopes?**<br>The plants are a mixture of Mountain Mule Ears and Arrowleaf Balsam Root.<br>**Where can I see wildlife?**<br>Wildlife at Lassen is just that--wild. Many of the larger mammals aren't seen very often with the exception of mule deer. Bears can occasionally be spotted along a hike. Golden-Mantled Ground Squirrels and chipmunks are a common sighting. Birding opportunities are best at Manzanita Lake and Summit Lake. Remember, feeding wildlife in Lassen is strictly prohibited.<br>**What are the butterflies all over the top of Lassen Peak and why are they there?**<br>They are California Tortoiseshell. They are carried by updrafts on the wind and eventually work their way down off of the peak.<br>**Where is the volcano?**<br>The park is filled with volcanoes. Many of them can be seen along hikes and along the drive through the park. There are hiking trails that climb volcanoes. For the most well known volcano in the park, see the next question.<br>**When did Lassen Peak last erupt?**<br>Lassen had a series of eruptions between 1914 and 1915 with minor steam eruptions through 1921.<br>**What are the four types of volcanoes?**<br>Plug Dome (Lassen Peak), Cinder Cone (Cinder Cone), Shield (Prospect Peak), and Composite (Mount Tehama).<br>**Why does the moss grow on all sides of the trees, not just the north side?**<br>The organism growing on the bark of the trees is actually staghorn lichen. The lichen is very delicate and slow growing.<br>**Does the lichen harm the trees?**<br>No, instead it uses the bark as an anchor to live. It derives micronutrients from the air.<br>**What is lichen?**<br>It is two organisms living symbiotically. It is a mix of algae which provides the food for the plant and a fungus which gives it structure.<br>**Where can I see fall colors?**<br>The best locations include: along the main road between Hot Rock and Devastated Area where aspens show off yellows and golds, Paradise Meadows, Hat Lake, Kings Creek Meadows, and the eastern shore of Butte Lake.<br>## Other<br>**How far is the drive through the park, how long does it take, and what can be seen along the route?**<br>The drive is 30 miles one way and takes about 1 hour without stops. The highway reaches a high point at an elevation of 8500 ft. at the base of Lassen Peak. Numerous pull-outs are available for stopping to take in views and photograph. Two short walks are available from the park highway: Sulphur Works, one mile north, and Devastated Area, 20 miles north, from the southwest entrance. A road guide is available for purchase at the visitor center and at the Loomis Plaza store.<br>**Why are there no guard rails on the park highway?**<br>The park highway is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Because of that designation, improvements that alter the historic qualities of the road are prohibited and there have been few major modifications to meet with current highway standards (passing lanes, emergency lanes, guard rails, etc). |
