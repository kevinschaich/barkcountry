## Tahoe National Forest

## Dogs

The Tahoe National Forest is dog friendly with 
abundant and varied recreation opportunities for your 
enjoyment as a dog owner. Consider the information 
below if you plan on taking your dog on a forest 
adventure.

### Dogs on the Tahoe National Forest

As you are pack for your hike, camping trip, or other 
outing, please consider what would happen if you 
became separated from your dog. Be sure to have 
accurate identification and vaccination tags on his 
collar. If you become separated and cannot find your 
dog, call local authorities such as county animal 
control and veterinarians to see if anyone has found 
your dog. If you are at a campground or other 
facility, inform the campground host. The best way to 
avoid this situation is to keep your dog on a leash or 
under voice command at all times.

#### Your Packing List Should Include

• 6-foot leash

• Identification

• Pick-up bags

#### Basic Dog Owner Rules

Dispose of pet waste responsibly.

Owners must be available to take immediate control 
of their pet at all times.

In most areas, a leash is strongly recommended and 
may be required by County leash laws.

Be aware that dogs off-leash may threaten wildlife or 
present a hazard to the safety or convenience of other 
visitors.

Dogs in developed recreation sites are required to be 
leashed at all times.

#### County Rules in the National Forest Land

**Nevada:** Dogs do not have to be on a leash in the 
forest but must always be under direct control of the 
owner.

**Placer:** Dogs must be under immediate control of the 
owner or on a leash at all times.

**Sierra:** Dogs must always be under immediate 
control of the owner.

**Eldorado and Yuba:** Dogs are required to be kept 
on a leash at all times.

#### Please Be Aware

Dogs noses are sensitive to the sun, so be sure to 
apply sunscreen to their noses as often as you do for 
yourself.

Dogs can become injured and may develop very 
sensitive paws after long hikes, so be sure your dog is 
capable of handling the elements.

Ticks are abundant in the Tahoe National Forest. be 
sure to check your animals for ticks to avoid Lyme 
disease.

Giardia can be present in Sierra streams and lakes.  
Use caution and watch for symptoms when letting 
your pet drink the water from lakes and streams.

Consider whether you can carry your dog back to 
your starting point if he becomes injured or tired.

#### Dogs in Granite Chief Wilderness

Dogs are prohibited from May 15<sup>th</sup> through July 15<sup>th</sup>
each year in the Granite Chief Wilderness in the 
following deer fawning areas: The Wilderness 
portion of the French Meadows Game Refuge 
(except for the area east of the Pacific Crest Trail), 
the Big Springs Trail, and the portion of Five Lakes 
Creek that parallels that trail.
