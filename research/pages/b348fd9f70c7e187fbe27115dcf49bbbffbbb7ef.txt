| ![A visitor walking their leashed dog in parking lot](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/images/Joe-R-at-EG-parking-lot-dog_1.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)<br>NPS Photo<br>## Where can I Bring My Pet?<br>The well-being of your pets and park wildlife is of the utmost importance to us. For the safety of all, pets are allowed in only a few select areas, and may not be left unattended. Your pet must be leashed or physically restrained at all times. Please clean up after your pet and dispose of waste properly. <br>Pets are allowed:<br>- on the road to Exit Glacier (the Herman Leirer Road).<br>- in the parking lot of the Exit Glacier Nature Center. <br>### Pets are NOT allowed:<br>- on trails, such as the trails of the [Exit Glacier](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/exit-glacier-area.htm) area or the [Harding Icefield Trail](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/harding_icefield_trail.htm).<br>- in the park [campground](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/campgrounds.htm) or [public-use cabins](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/public-use-cabins.htm).<br>- in the [coastal backcountry](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/camping-and-landing-beaches.htm).<br>  <br>  - Pets are not allowed along the coast in an area extending from the mean high tide line to 1/4 mile inland from 5/30 - 11/1.<br>- in any park building. <br>## Service Animals<br>Service animals are allowed in all facilities (including public-use cabins and campgrounds) and on trails unless an area has been closed by the superintendent to protect park resources.<br>The [2010 revision to Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act](https://www.ada.gov/service_animals_2010.htm) (ADA) defines a service animal "as a dog that has been individually trained to do work or perform tasks for the benefit of an individual with a disability, including a physical, sensory, psychiatric, intellectual, or other mental disability." Dogs which are not trained to perform tasks that mitigate the effects of a disability, including dogs that are used to purely provide comfort or emotional support (therapy animals) are considered pets.<br>## Dog Mushing / Skijoring<br>Dogs used for mushing or skijoring may be used on the Harding Icefield at any time, and in the Exit Glacier area when the road is [closed due to snow](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/winter-activities.htm) and vehicle access is not allowed.<br>## Learn More<br>[![Two people, one in wheelchair, look at directions on sign](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/grid_builder/kefj/crop16_9/7633AAC4-E58C-C692-74B4D997335CD988.jpg?width=640&quality=90&mode=crop)\<br>\<br>Accessibility](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/accessibility.htm) <br>Learn about accessibility considerations at Kenai Fjords.<br>[![A sow and cub cross a paved trail](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/grid_builder/kefj/crop16_9/767E49BF-AFE1-8465-458588127ADFFC8A.jpg?width=640&quality=90&mode=crop)\<br>\<br>Wildlife Safety](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/wildlife-safety.htm) <br>Help keep Kenai Fjords' animals wild by learning what to do when encountering wildlife.<br>[![Three snowmobiles in front of a snow-covered glacier](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/grid_builder/kefj/crop16_9/766901F1-09AD-0E24-F6D99A1234A3E9E1.jpg?width=640&quality=90&mode=crop)\<br>\<br>Rules & Regulations](https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/rules-and-regulations.htm) <br>There are a variety of rules and regulations that help us protect park resources and provide visitors with a safe and enjoyable experience. |
