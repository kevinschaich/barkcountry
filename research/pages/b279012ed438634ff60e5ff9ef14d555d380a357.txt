# Forest Service encouraging safe recreation over Labor Day weekend in Pacific Northwest

**Release Date:** September 4, 2026

**(Portland, OR, September 4, 2026) —** As the Labor Day holiday approaches, the Forest Service encourages visitors to plan ahead and check current fire restrictions and area closures in place due to active wildfires.

While significant wetting rain is in the Pacific Northwest forecast, there are currently 25 large fires burning in Oregon and Washington, and in 2026 so far, more than 2.4 million acres in Oregon and nearly 900,000 acres in Washington have burned across various land ownerships.

Despite widespread closures, many recreation opportunities remain open across Oregon and Washington. Land managers recommend planning alternate destinations and staying flexible, as conditions and evacuation levels can change quickly. Visitors should “Know Before You Go” by reviewing current closures, fire restrictions, and road conditions before leaving home and by preparing backup plans in case sites become inaccessible.

For current information on fire conditions, visitors should check local ranger district websites or contact a local office directly.  A detailed list of current forest closures and alerts is available online at: [https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/safety-ethics/alerts-forest-oregon-and-washington](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/safety-ethics/alerts-forest-oregon-and-washington)

To support safe and responsible recreation, the following tips are provided for activities throughout the holiday weekend:

**Stay Informed & Plan Ahead**

- Check for fire restrictions or closures before heading out.

- Sunrise and sunset are ideal for hikes—avoid heat peaks which can cause heat-related illnesses.

- Share your itinerary with someone, travel in groups, carry reliable maps (mobile service may be unavailable), and prepare for emergencies.

**Hydration & Heat Safety**

- Prepare for heat by bringing ample water, applying sunscreen, and dressing for hot conditions.

**Fire Prevention**

- Campfires: observe local restrictions; build only in designated areas, clear a 5 foot bare-soil perimeter, and extinguish completely — ‘drown, stir, feel’ before leaving.

- No fireworks are allowed—leave them at home.

- Vehicle safety: do not park on dry grass—hot components can spark fires.

**Vegetation & Wildlife Safety**

- Be cautious of dead or hazard trees; avoid camping under them.

- Recreate responsibly in fire burn areas. Burned landscapes present numerous safety hazards that either did not exist prior to the fire or have been worsened by the effects of the fire. Be on the lookout for burned or falling trees and limbs, ash pits, burned stump holes, and flash flooding.

- Pack out all trash; clean up after yourself to protect wildlife and water systems.

**Pet and eBike Etiquette**

- Respect dog rules—many beaches and wilderness zones prohibit dogs.

- Ride eBikes only on designated motorized trails. Be courteous to others.

**Water-Related Dangers**

- Cold water shock is a hazard—wear life jackets during swimming or boating.

- Check the forecast and be aware of flash flooding risk, especially in burned areas.

**Traffic & Parking**

- Expect crowded roads; consider carpooling, biking, or public transport.

- Avoid parking on vegetation—risking spark-started fires.

- Near active fires, visitors can expect to encounter firefighter traffic, dusty roads, and areas of smoke. Use caution and follow the designated speed limit when driving on forest roads.

**Report wildfires, illegal fireworks, or unsafe fire use by calling 911.**
