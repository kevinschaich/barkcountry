# Summer travel season begins May 21 on the White River National Forest

## Visitors urged to stay off muddy roads and trails

**Release Date:** May 19, 2026

**Glenwood Springs, CO** \- –With the summer motorized and mountain bike travel season beginning May 21 in most areas on the White River National Forest, forest officials are urging visitors to avoid muddy roads.

“Conditions vary, and not every road opening for the summer season on May 21 will be dry or passable, especially given the high number of trees that blew down over the winter and the recent moisture,” said Recreation Program Manager Sam Massman. “We’re asking visitors to please be patient while we work on clearing the downed trees and to avoid using muddy roads and trails until they have time to dry out and harden to minimize damage.”

Some roads and trails opened early this year due to dry conditions; however, many are not scheduled to open until later in May or June because of snow at higher elevations or to reduce disturbance to wildlife such as calving elk.

E-bikes are considered motorized transportation on National Forest System lands and may only be ridden on roads and trails designated open to motorized vehicles on the White River National Forest. Traditional (non e-bike) bicycles are allowed on designated trails and roads where mechanized use is permitted.

Off-road and off-trail travel is prohibited for all motorized and mechanized vehicles on the White River National Forest.

All forest visitors are responsible for knowing when and where they can drive or ride. Ranger district offices have the latest site-specific information. Current conditions and summer motor vehicle use and mountain bike maps are also available at: [www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver).

Most developed campgrounds on the White River National Forest open this month as well. The White River National Forest website includes information about all its campgrounds, and reservations for most are available at [www.recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/).

Currently the Lowry and Prospector Campgrounds along with Windy Point Group Site at Dillon Reservoir do not have water because the well that serves them is dry this spring. Campers should bring their own water.

“The wells that serve our campgrounds may be starting with less water due to the light snowpack, so we’re asking all campers to conserve water,” said Recreation Program Manager Paula Peterson. “This includes filling up any holding tanks in RVs at home rather than at the campground so that our water supplies last the whole season.”

All visitors to the White River National Forest should check the forest’s website for any updates or fire restrictions. In areas where fires are allowed, visitors should be careful with fire and ensure their campfire is cold to the touch before leaving camp.
