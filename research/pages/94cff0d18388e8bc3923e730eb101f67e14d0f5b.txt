# Animals

The Savannah River Site is one of the Southeast’s most remarkable conservation landscapes. Spanning more than 198,000 acres of protected forests, wetlands, and river corridors, SRS offers habitats found nowhere else in such close proximity.

[![Newly hatched Red cockaded woodpecker being held.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/medium/public/r08/savannah-river/image/1001279030%20%282%29.jpg?itok=3NKQXFmH)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r08/savannah-river/image/1001279030%20%282%29.jpg)

A newly hatched Red Cockaded Woodpecker being held in preparation for banding to aid in future identification.

_(Joe Orosz)_

Our longleaf pine and mixed hardwood forests support many familiar species—white‑tailed deer, eastern gray squirrels, wild turkeys, and the elusive bobcat. With a careful eye, you may spot the federally endangered red‑cockaded woodpecker, a species that depends on the mature pines we work hard to restore and maintain. Gopher tortoises, another conservation priority here, create burrows that support an entire community of reptiles, amphibians, and insects.

Wetlands, swamps, and Carolina bays add another layer of richness to the landscape. These areas are alive with river otters, beavers, frogs, and a wide variety of wading birds, including great egrets and wood storks. The American alligator, once threatened, is now a conservation success story and can often be seen basking along quiet pond edges.

Along the Savannah River, bald eagles, ospreys, and migratory waterfowl take advantage of abundant fish populations. Seasonal changes bring waves of songbirds, butterflies, and pollinators that highlight the site’s ecological rhythms throughout the year.

The wildlife of the Savannah River Site offers countless opportunities for discovery. Every trail, wetland overlook, and forest edge reveals another part of this dynamic ecosystem—one that continues to thrive thanks to decades of careful management and conservation partnerships.
