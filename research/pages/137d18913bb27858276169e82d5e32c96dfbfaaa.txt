# A guide to understanding regulations in the Bighorn National Forest

_Sara Evans Kirol_,
_Bighorn National Forest_

_December 22, 2025_

The Bighorn National Forest is a wonderful place to visit year-round. Before you start planning that adventure, it is important to understand the regulations of the forest for the protection of yourself and special habitats, sites and research areas.

Before heading out, make sure to obtain a free Bighorn National Forest [motor vehicle use map](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn/maps-guides/motor-vehicle-use-map). This map has detailed information on route opening and closing dates, as well as off-road travel. The [Alerts](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn/alerts) page on the Bighorn National Forest website contains special orders for the forest. This is where you will find any fire restrictions, camping regulations, site closures and more.

The Bighorn National Forest [visitor map](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn/maps-guides/forest-visitor-map) is also an important item with regulatory information as well as helpful details on where to go. This map shows area-wide provisions known as “S Areas,” which stands for:

- **S1: natural research areas**. These special places form a network of ecological reserves designated for research and education and the maintenance of biological diversity. Motorized use is prohibited year-round in these areas.
- **S2/3: crucial habitat areas.** S2 areas are closed annually to motorized use from Nov. 16 through May 15 to protect wintering wildlife. S3 areas are set aside to allow wildlife to bear and raise their young without motorized disturbance from March 31 through Nov. 15 annually. These areas were designated during consultation with the Wyoming Game and Fish Department during the development of the forest’s current land and resource management plan.
- **S4: over snow vehicle recreation areas**. This includes all roads and trails in those locations. Over snow vehicles are prohibited from July 1 through Nov. 15.
- **S5: non-motorized recreation areas**. They include Nordic and downhill ski areas. Motorized use is prohibited from Dec. 1 through May 1.
- **S6: Medicine Wheel/Medicine Mountain National Historic Landmark site.** Due to the sensitive ecosystem and the cultural significance of this area there are special restrictions to protect both. No over snow vehicles are allowed in this area.

If you are an avid winter visitor, there are some additional regulations to be aware of:

- It is prohibited to use motorized wheeled vehicles on designated snowmobile trails from Dec. 15 through March 31. These trails are groomed regularly by the Wyoming State Trails program and wheeled vehicles could damage the established base creating hazards in the trail and require additional restoration work.
- Arrowhead Bowl off Highway 14 has area restrictions with a designated one-way motorized loop around a non-motorized area. This has been in effect for many years to prevent pedestrians sledding down the hill from collisions with motorized vehicles.
- There must be a minimum of six inches of snow on the ground to use over snow vehicles on Forest roads and trails year-round and off Forest roads and trails from Nov. 16 through May 15.
- Dogs are not allowed in the Sibley Nordic Ski Area when posted during the trail grooming season. This is to protect the set tracks and groomed ski trails from damage.

If you plan to do some camping while you are in the Bighorn National Forest, there is a 14-day stay limit year-round. Please note, personal property cannot be left unattended for more than 72 hours in national forests, nationwide unless designated otherwise. While on site, for your safety and the safety of wildlife, make sure to store all food, trash and other attractants in hard-sided covered vehicles, bear resistant containers or locked approved coolers to avoid attracting and habituating wildlife to humans. For more information on what counts as an approved container, visit the [Interagency Grizzly Bear Committee](https://igbconline.org/programs/bear-resistant-products/#1634661901884-4d0820a6-1a88) website.

The Bighorn National Forest is an amazing place throughout the year, but it takes us all to ensure it remains that way. Taking a little extra time to know the basics before you go will help make your overall experience a lot more positive. Be sure to watch the Bighorn National Forest’s [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/bighornnf) and [X](https://x.com/BighornUSFS) sites.
