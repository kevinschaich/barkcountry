[Overview](https://research.fs.usda.gov/srs/centers/catt#overview)

**The Center for Aquatic Technology Transfer**(CATT) is a science delivery program. CATT biologists and technicians are Southern Research Station (SRS) employees funded by the National Forest System (NFS). Guided by core values of communication, partnership, inclusion, accountability, and safety, we collaborate with the Forest Service science community and others to develop custom solutions for our project partners.

![CATT Techs](https://research.fs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/styles/480px_wide/public/2023-02/srs-catttechs.jpg?itok=jnswSOin)

Take a look at the variety of projects CATT was involved in last year: [CATT Fiscal Year 2024 Report](https://research.fs.usda.gov/srs/understory/catt-2024-annual-report "CATT 2024 Annual Report").

The CATT was created in 1995 in response to the growing need for research technologies to be applied directly to management challenges. The number of research personnel was, and still is, too small relative to the number of fisheries and aquatics resource managers to satisfy specific needs. Our mission is to increase the capacity of our partners through delivery of science-based support.

Full-time CATT personnel are stationed in Blacksburg, Virginia, and provide services throughout the United States.

Our focus is on aquatics related management challenges. Our flexible organizational structure allows us to rapidly develop and apply custom solutions to both short- and long-term projects. Past projects range from providing a field technician for an afternoon of fish sampling, to region-wide, multi-year efforts, including sampling design, personnel management, data analysis, and reporting.

[Jobs](https://research.fs.usda.gov/srs/centers/catt#jobs)

If you enjoy working in streams and rivers, want to make a positive impact on our national forests, and are seeking opportunities to contribute to science then we hope you will consider joining our team!

**When is CATT hiring?** We are hiring now! Submit your resume today and we will contact you to discuss our current job openings and your availability.

**I am a current undergraduate student. Can I apply?** Yes! Some of our positions are short-term, during the traditional college summer break. Our short-term jobs are great for gaining hands-on fisheries field work experience and developing teamwork skills.

**Does CATT offer year-round positions?** Yes! We offer positions that allow you to work year-round for up to 1,500 hours per year. Our year-round positions provide for flexible work schedules while you gain fisheries field work experience and develop leadership skills.

**What type of work will I do with CATT?** Some days you may wade miles of national forest streams to assess habitat quality, other days you may assess fish growth with a tagging study, and some days you might share the underwater world with visitors through a snorkeling program. We never know what might be coming next, but we know you won’t be bored! Check out our website for more information about our recent projects.

**Where is CATT and will I get to travel?** Our home office is in Blacksburg, VA, but most of our work has us travelling to national forests, national parks, and adjoining lands throughout the Southeast. We provide fleet vehicles, lodging, and money to cover meals while you are traveling.

**Does CATT provide networking opportunities?** In addition to our full-time CATT biologists, you will work with natural resource professionals from state and federal agencies, university researchers, graduate students, and others. Your work can span from the mountains, to piedmont, to coastal plain allowing you to explore a wide range of work settings and opportunities.

**Will my work with CATT make a difference?** Absolutely! Our teams collect information used directly by national forest managers to protect vulnerable aquatic animals and identify high-impact stream restoration projects. Our teams contribute to cutting-edge scientific research needed to better understand and manage our nation’s waters.

**How can I prepare myself for a CATT job?** Interest in the outdoors, enthusiasm, and positive attitude are the foundation for success in your CATT job! CATT employees hike several miles per day with backpacks in rocky streams, so stay fit. Working around water means swimming ability is a must. CATT employees have a valid drivers license with less than 2 accidents and less than 3 moving violations in the past 4 years.

**How do I apply?** Simple! Email a resume to [colin.w.krause@usda.gov](mailto:colin.w.krause@usda.gov). We will be in touch.
