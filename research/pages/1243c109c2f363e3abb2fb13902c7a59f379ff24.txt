# Wildlife Safety Awareness

_Sara Evans Kirol_,
_Rocky Mountain Region, Bighorn National Forest_

_July 14, 2025_

The Bighorn National Forest is a great place to visit year-round but especially during the warmer seasons.  There are amazing views, ample recreational opportunities, and abundant wildlife.  It’s hard to spend a day in the Bighorns without seeing several different animal species. However, locals and visitors alike should be cautious around wildlife.

To help avoid conflicts with wildlife that could cause you, your pet, or a loved one injury, try these actions.

Avoid getting between a mother animal and her young and always keep pets leashed or under control. This will require you to be aware of your surroundings, especially when walking through areas near rivers, creeks, and ponds that have thick vegetation. Moose frequent these areas for cover and to find their favorite foods.

Because it may be difficult to spot another animal in the thick brush or dense trees, you may not have much time to react. To alert animals of your presence and to reduce surprises, make a little noise or wear bells as you fish or hike.  Look around often and pay attention to your surroundings.

Consider carrying bear spray.  This non-lethal spray contains high concentrations of capsaicin, think super-spicy chili pepper oil in your eyes, and is designed to deter charging grizzly bears.  It also works on other animals such as moose or mountain lions.

Bears are always searching for food and are curious, intelligent animals that have great memories. Their eyesight is similar to humans, but their sense of smell is seven times more powerful than a blood hound’s, enabling them to smell food from miles away. Those are the very traits that can sometimes get them, and us, into trouble. Most bears are wary of humans and try to avoid them. However, when they are allowed to access human food and garbage, they can learn to associate people with food and be tenacious in their pursuit of something to eat, even if humans are around. This is when things can get dangerous.

There is a wildlife attractant storage requirement in the Bighorn National Forest and food storage lockers are provided at many campsites. If one is available, it should be used to store attractants.  If there is not one available, all wildlife attractants must be stored in a manner that wildlife cannot access them. Visit the [occupancy and use Forest Special Order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn/alerts/forest-special-order-02-02-24-02-occupancy-and-use-camping-regulations) page on our website for more details. Always give wildlife a lot of space to reduce stress and prevent a possible defensive reaction.  If you see an animal behaving as if it is agitated or it is a mother with young, leave the area or take a different route to avoid them.

Your chances of encountering a mountain lion are small. However, try not to hike alone and avoid excursions at dawn and dusk. Keep small children close or carry them, as they can be seen as prey to mountain lions. Never approach a lion and always give it a clear way to escape without confrontation. Avoid running away and stimulating a mountain lion’s instinct to chase. Make yourself appear big- stand tall and hold up your arms or open your jacket wide, if you’re wearing one.

Make good choices and recreate responsibly so that your experience is a good one.  For more details on wildlife safety visit the Wyoming Game and Fish Department’s [Bear Wise](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/wyoming-wildlife/large-carnivore/bear-wise-wyoming) and [mountain lion safety](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/wyoming-wildlife/large-carnivore/mountain-lions-wyoming) pages.  The Bighorn National Forest’s web page is another resource with information [https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/bighorn).  Or check out our Facebook ( [https://www.facebook.com/bighornnf](https://www.facebook.com/bighornnf)) and X  ( [https://x.com/BighornUSFS](https://x.com/BighornUSFS)) social media platforms.
