# Dogs on Inyo National Forest

On Inyo National Forest, dogs must be on a leash year-round in **developed recreation sites** which are defined as “an area which has been improved or developed for recreation.” Developed recreation sites include areas such as campgrounds, picnic and day use areas.

This law can be found in the Code of Federal Regulations, [Title 36 - Parks, Forests, and Public Property](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36), [CHAPTER II - FOREST SERVICE, DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/chapter-II), [PART 261 - PROHIBITIONS](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/part-261), [Subpart A - General Prohibitions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/part-261/subpart-A):

**§ 261.16 Developed** [**recreation**](https://www.law.cornell.edu/definitions/index.php?width=840&height=800&iframe=true&def_id=c8f98070c86b30de424ac53f2abac281&term_occur=999&term_src=Title:36:Chapter:II:Part:261:Subpart:A:261.16) **sites.**

The following are prohibited:

**(j)** Bringing in or possessing an animal, other than a service animal, unless it is crated, caged, or upon a leash not longer than six feet, or otherwise under physical restrictive control.

**(k)** Bringing in or possessing in a swimming area an animal, other than a service animal. There are no recreation sites on the Inyo National Forest that are being managed as designated swimming areas.

Outside of developed recreation sites, dogs may or may not be required to be on a leash, depending on local county or city ordinances (see below). However, dog owners could be subject to other violations such as **§ 261.8 Fish and Wildlife:**

**(d)** Possessing a dog not on a leash or otherwise confined.

This means if your dog is not being physically restrained on a leash and is harassing wildlife, you could be cited. The only exception for this is when the dog is in pursuit of a game animal and the owner has the required hunting permits.

## Local City and County Laws

**The Town of Mammoth Lakes** requires dogs to be leashed within city limits, a 4-square mile Municipal Boundary which includes the Lakes Basin, Mammoth Pass, Mammoth Crest to Crystal Lake, Horseshoe Lake, part of Sherwin Creek and more. [**Interactive map**](https://www.townofmammothlakes.ca.gov/832/Dog-Services#:~:text=in%20rescue%20actions.-,6.12.,the%20owner%20of%20the%20dog.)

[**Mono County**](https://library.municode.com/ca/mono_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT9AN_CH9.36PRAC) **:**

Mono County does not require a dog to be on a leash except on private properties and in these unincorporated areas:

June Lake, Hilton Creek, Mammoth-June Lake Airport, White Mountain Estates, Lee Vining, Wheeler Crest, Chalfant, Sunny Slopes and Pine Glade. See [Title 9: Animals, Chapter 9.44 - Dog Regulations](https://library.municode.com/ca/mono_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT9AN_CH9.36PRAC) for more thorough descriptions of these areas.

[**Inyo County**](https://ecode360.com/44462784#44462784) **:**

Inyo County does not require a dog to be on a leash except for “densely populated areas” (8.20.180):

The Inyo National Forest also contains parts of five other counties within its boundaries: Madera, Fresno, and Tulare Counties in California, and Esmeralda and Mineral Counties in Nevada.

**Madera, Fresno, and Tulare Counties:**

These counties requiredogs to be leashed within its boundaries so please be aware while present with your dog in the western edges of the forest.

- [**Madera County**](https://library.municode.com/ca/madera_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT6ANAG_CH6.04ANCO_IIILIVA_6.04.290KELIEQ) includes Reds Meadow, the Minarets and the Thousand Island Lakes area. Check their [interactive boundary map](https://gis.maderacounty.com/portal/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=6f052c128f524f0a90ed280e1a248d58) to see if where you’re hiking requires your dog to be leashed in the backcountry.
- [**Fresno County**](http://library.municode.com/ca/fresno_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT9AN_CH9.04DOLICO_9.04.250RULA) includes Duck Lake, McGee Pass and other areas.
- [**Tulare County**](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/tulare/latest/tulare_ca/0-0-0-29707#JD_6.12.310) includes Kennedy Meadows, Monache Meadows and part of the Golden Trout Wilderness.
- Check [this interactive map](https://iportal.tularecounty.ca.gov/iportal/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=3ddb0eb36a7e488499c2dbe559286d33) with both Fresno and Tulare Counties in it.

[**Esmerelda County, NV**](https://www.animallaw.info/statute/nv-leash-law-wildlife-management-area): It is unlawful for the owner of any dog to permit such dog to run at large if such dog is actively tracking, pursuing, harassing, attacking or killing any wildlife in a state-owned wildlife management area. Includes Boundary Peak area.

[**Mineral County, NV**](http://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/mineralcountynv/latest/mineral_nv/0-0-0-1514) **:** It is unlawful and punishable as a misdemeanor for the owner of any dog whether licensed or unlicensed to suffer, permit or allow such dog to run at large on any public street, alley, park, square or place, or in any vacant or unenclosed lot within populated areas of the county. Any dog shall be deemed running at large in a populated area unless led or restrained by a substantial leash, chain, strap or cord not more than six feet (6') in length attached to its collar, and held by some person or made fast to some object, except that a dog restrained on the property of the owner of such dog may be restrained by a fence, leash, chain, strap or cord of such length as not to extend beyond the boundary line of the property. A dog shall be deemed running at large in an unpopulated area if not with the owner and not under the voice control of the owner. (MC Ord. 102A § 2, 1986)

## Dog Waste

Let’s face it: encountering dog waste anywhere in the forest while you’re trying to enjoy it is downright disgusting. Not only that, it contaminates the water resources you’re trying to enjoy further downstream.

Furthermore, you may be cited by federal officers for failing to dispose of your pet’s waste.

Under 36 CFR [**§ 261.11 Sanitation:**](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/section-261.11)

(b) Possessing or leaving refuse, debris, or litter in an exposed or unsanitary condition.

(c) Placing in or near a stream, lake, or other water any substance which does or may pollute a stream, lake, or other water.

(d) Failing to dispose of all garbage, including any paper, can, bottle, sewage, waste water or material, or rubbish either by removal from the site or area, or by depositing it into receptacles or at places provided for such purposes.

Contrary to what some might claim, dog waste is not a natural part of the forest and must immediately be removed or buried properly. Here’s how:

- Always keep a stash of poop bags in various pockets of your backpack and on your leash itself.
- Find a container of some sort that keeps the smell out if you’re day hiking. Some companies have created pouches with liners specifically for this purpose.
- Backpacking or staying out for a very long day and don’t want to carry your pet’s waste with you? Bring a shovel and bury it according to the same [Leave No Trace](https://lnt.org/why/7-principles/dispose-of-waste-properly/) principals you would with your own waste: at least 6 inches deep and 200 feet away from any body of water, trail, or camp (this is where the shovel really comes in handy for carrying the waste away from where your pet has decided to do its business! Many times, they are adjacent to a water source).

Please keep in mind, the better you keep your dog’s waste out of the forest, the more pristine the forest will stay for generations to come!

## Dog Etiquette

- Wherever you are with your dog, we highly recommend that you leash them, especially in crowded and high-use trails such as Little Lakes Trail in Rock Creek, Big Pine Lakes trails, and the Lakes Basin area trails.
- Leashing your dog can mean the difference between having a great trip in the Inyo National Forest or a horrifying one. Lately the forest has received many complaints of visitors encountering off-leash dogs on the trail.
- You can protect your canine companion and your experience by keeping your dog on a leash while visiting public lands and encouraging others to do the same.
- Both leashed _and_ unleashed dogs often trigger fearful reactions for other hikers and their dogs, causing unwanted interactions that can even turn dangerous for all those involved. If the dogs are leashed, the situation can be better controlled.

### What are some other reasons to leash?

- Keep it from getting lost.
- Enhance your awareness of nearby wildlife or other visitors with your dog’s key senses.
- Keep better track of where/when they go.
- Help decrease negative visitor experiences for others.

## FAQs and Myths

- **“My dog is nice so he/she doesn’t need to be on a leash.”**
  - Your dog may be nice, but the other dogs, humans and horses you encounter may often perceive another dog moving toward them as a threat. They will react out of uncertainty and defensively, _whether or not_ they are on a leash, and the resulting interaction can be unintentionally negative.
- **“What about non-developed sites like on trails?”**
  - The Forest does not have any federal laws requiring dogs on leash, however if you are hiking within city or counties that do require them, you must follow their respective ordinances.
- **“Who’s going to enforce the leash laws?”**
  - U.S. Forest Service law enforcement officers have the approved authority to cite city or county ordinances within Mono County, including those in Mammoth Lakes.
  - Other forest staff who have Forest Protection Officer qualifications have the authority to enforce the CFR laws regarding developed recreation areas and fish and wildlife.
    - Example: Wilderness ranger working at thousand island lakes can cite them under the CFR 261.8 leash law because it is backed by the leash laws in Madera County, but not in other counties where there are no leash laws.
  - Bureau of Land Management law enforcement officers also have the authority to enforce the federal CFRs in addition to city and county ordinances.
  - State of California Fish and Game Wardens can enforce only state and city ordinances, but not federal law.
- **“Where can I report dogs and owners that have attacked me?”**
  - Contact the respective county animal control or city to file a report.
