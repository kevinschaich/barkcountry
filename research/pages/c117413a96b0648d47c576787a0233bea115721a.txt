# Wapusk National Park

Let Wapusk National Park's expansive wilderness fill you with awe. This 11,475-square-kilometre park, at the transition between boreal forest and arctic tundra, protects one of the largest polar bear maternity denning areas in the world.

Visits to Wapusk National Park are primarily done via licensed tour operators in Churchill. **A [licensed tour operator](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/mb/wapusk/visit/services#licensedtouroperators) is required for all activities in the park.**

Wapusk National Park is on Treaty 5 territory and the lands traditionally used by the Cree, Dene, Inuit, and Red River Métis peoples.

## More places to discover with Parks Canada

[![](https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/WET4/proximite-nearby/mb/princeofwalesfort-640x360.jpg?modified=20221126220835)](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/mb/prince)

### [Prince of Wales Fort National Historic Site](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/mb/prince)

Stone remains of the star-shaped, 18th century Prince of Wales Fort stand on Hudson Bay’s tundra shore, offering a glimpse into a remote Hudson’s Bay outpost amid a wild subarctic landscape alive with polar bears, birds and beluga whales.

[![](https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/WET4/proximite-nearby/mb/yorkfactory-640x360.jpg?modified=20221126220836)](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/mb/yorkfactory)

### [York Factory National Historic Site](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/mb/yorkfactory)

Perched incongruously amid vast tundra wilderness, York Factory was the Hudson’s Bay Company’s major trading and administration centre for centuries. Its grounds and grand Depot, filled with artifacts, provide profound insights into a pivotal period in Canada’s founding days.
