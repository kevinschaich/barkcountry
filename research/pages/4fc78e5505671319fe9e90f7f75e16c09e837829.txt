# Welcome to Bridger-Teton National Forest

Located in Western Wyoming, the Bridger-Teton National Forest offers more than 3.4 million acres of public land for your enjoyment. With its pristine watersheds, abundant wildlife, and immense wildlands, the Bridger-Teton comprises a large part of the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, the largest intact ecosystem in the lower 48 states. Offering nearly 1.2 million acres of designated Wilderness, over 3,000 miles of roads and trails, and thousands of miles of unspoiled rivers and streams, the Bridger-Teton offers something for everyone. Visit this beautiful landscape and experience your forest.

![A clean camp with tent, camping chairs, fire pit, and bear safety sign](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r04/bridger-teton/image/445027120_856540139850455_8206911835104443365_n.jpg?h=efc07e25)

Camping is a great way to immerse yourself in the stunning scenery of the Bridger-Teton National Forest. There are many options for camping across the 3.4 million acre forest. If you're feeling adventurous and searching for solitude, try dispersed camping. Looking for a few more comforts? Tent camp, car camp, or park your recreational vehicle at a developed campground. You can even reserve a Forest guard station cabin.

[Learn more](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/recreation/camping-cabins)

![Recreationists in a raft on the Snake River](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r04/bridger-teton/image/Snake-%20Lunch%20Counter-%20evening%20-%20Tom%20OKeefe.jpeg?h=a13e2dd6)

From May 1 through Nov. 1, Snake River users have the option to purchase a day pass via electronic pay station kiosks at Snake River boat ramps with a credit or debit card or they can purchase a season pass on [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/activitypass/AP23881).

Timed launch reservations for large non-commercial groups of 16-30 people are also available on [Recreation.gov](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/10112505) for May 1 through Oct. 1.

[Learn more](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/permits/snake-river-permits)

![Grizzly Bear Standing Up](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/bridger-teton/Grizzly%20Bear%20Standing%20Up.jpg?h=c3635fa2)

For the safety of humans and bears alike, the Bridger-Teton National Forest is committed to providing readily available information to Forest visitors about food storage regulations. This will help minimize negative interactions between people and bears when recreating in bear country. We encourage you to take the pledge to educate yourself for your safety and the safety of bears!

[Learn More](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/safety-ethics/be-bear-wise-keep-bears-wild-people-safe)

![Squaretop Mountain and forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r04/bridger-teton/image/image_123650291_0.JPG?h=501361cb)

The Bridger-Teton National Forest began the process to revise its forest plan. Forest plans function similarly to county comprehensive plans and zoning documents, and provide comprehensive, strategic, and integrated resource direction that guide all decisions about future projects.

Help shape the future of the Bridger-Teton National Forest so together we can care for the land and serve people for generations to come. Stay tuned for upcoming events and additional information.
