# Cha'chaany Hamuk Trailhead -- Three Sisters Falls

Current Conditions

TRAIL DANGER & HEAT EXHAUSTION
This trail features an inverted profile: you start by hiking down into the canyon and must hike out going completely uphill. Many hikers exhaust their water and physical energy at the waterfall pools, failing to plan for the strenuous ascent under direct, unshaded sunlight. Rescues due to heat related injuries are frequently seen in this area.

Welcome to the Cha’chaany Hamuk Trailhead! Cha’chaany Hamuk is the Kumeyaay name for “Three Waters.” Kumeyaay utilized the native plants and resources in many ways for daily living. The San Diego River continues to be a significant water resource supporting wildlife, supplying water for local communities, and providing a wilderness experience for forest user.

Gaining significantly in popularity, this frequently crowded, yet highly challenging hike takes adventurers through several beautiful ecosystems before leading to the main event: three large, tiered waterfalls wedged between tall, rocky ravines.

Access to the falls and the neighboring Eagle Peak is through the fully reconstructed Three Sisters Trail (3E04). This challenging trail descends into Boulder Creek canyon, passing through different ecosystems, and giving hikers views of the falls and rocky cliffs below Eagle Peak. The trail approaches the falls from a canyon side wall and ends above the lowest of three pools.

Eagle Peak Trail (3E05) is an upland trail that has glimpses of Three Sisters falls, views from rocky cliffs, and a spectacular 360 vista of the San Diego River Watershed, Boulder Creek, and the surrounding uplands at the terminus. Access to Eagle Peak trail from a junction off Three Sisters trail at a saddle approximately 1 ½ mile from the trailhead. After the junction, your climb will rise and fall along a rocky ridgeline overlooking brushy slopes with scattered oaks until you reach the peak.

If you do not feel you are ready for the intensity of this hike, please reference our [hiking page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/cleveland/recreation/opportunities/hiking) for other trail opportunities.

**Quick Facts & Trail Specifications**

**Distance**

4.5 miles (Round Trip to Three Sister Falls)

Varies, depending on whether hiking to the upper waterfall or traversing the rocky bottom. Three Sisters Trail (3E04) round trip is 4.5 miles. Eagle Peak Trail (3E05) round trip is 3.0 miles.

**Elevation Change**

1,000 feet

The steep elevation gain occurs entirely on the return trip back up to the trailhead.

**Difficulty Rating**

Moderate to Strenuous

Increased difficulty with high heat conditions. Shade is limited, injuries and heat illness are common. Not advised for those who are not regularly active.

**Hike Time**

Full Day / Allow 3–4 Hours

Recommend allocating a full day; average moving time is roughly 2.5 to 3 hours, excluding time spent at the falls.

**Best Seasons**

Winter and Spring

Peak flow occurs after winter rains. The falls typically dry up entirely by mid-to-late summer.

## Getting There

### Latitude / Longitude

**Latitude:** 32.9845

**Longitude:** -116.6789

### Directions

Situated between Santa Ysabel and Descanso, CA. The road leading to Cha’chaany Hamuk Trailhead is a county maintained road with unpaved sections – ( via Boulder Road)

### Parking

Hardpack gravel parking lot is designed to accommodate standard vehicles.

Vehicles with trailers are not recommended.

## Facility and Amenity Information

### Restrooms

Two restroom buildings, each with two stalls/vault toilets, four toilets total.

### Water

Potable water is not available at this site.

Hikers must bring a minimum of 3 to 4 liters of water per person and pet.
