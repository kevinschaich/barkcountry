# Nááts'įhch'oh National Park Reserve

![](https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/pn-np/nt/woodbuffalo/2024-changes/01_11-nodrone/No-Drone_nobkgd.png)
Using recreational drones in Nááts'įhch'oh National Park Reserve is prohibited. Operating a drone within park boundaries may face law enforcement action and a fine of up to $25,000.

Nááts'įhch'oh National Park Reserve is named after Nááts'įhch'oh the mountain – a powerful place for the people of the Sahtu. Near the Yukon-Northwest Territories border, the park is in the traditional lands of the Shúhtaot'ine (Mountain Dene), and home to grizzly bear, Dall’s sheep, mountain goats, and woodland caribou.

The trips are for experienced paddlers only; in fact, only a handful of groups tackle the run each year. Guided tours are available from multiple outfitters.

### [Place names](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nt/naatsihchoh/info/culture/names_noms)

The dialect used for place names in the park is that of the Shúhtaot’ine (Mountain Dene). Language is a key to understanding the cultural landscape of Nááts’įhch’oh National Park Reserve.

[![](https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/pn-np/nt/naatsihchoh/2022-site-refresh/features/2022-A04T6817-640x360.jpg?modified=00010101000000)](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nt/naatsihchoh/visit)

### [Planning a Trip](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nt/naatsihchoh/visit)

Nááts'įhch'oh National Park Reserve is located in the southwest portion of the Northwest Territories. Most visitors access the park via floatplane from surrounding communities in the Northwest Territories and Yukon

### [Saoyú-ʔehdacho National Historic Site](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nt/saoyuehdacho)

Saoyú-Ɂehdacho National Historic Site celebrates the traditional lifestyles of the Sahtúgot’įnę – “the people of the Sahtú.” Visitors to Canada’s largest National Historic Site learn about the teaching, healing and spiritual places as conveyed through oral history.

### [Thaidene Nëné National Park Reserve](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nt/thaidene-nene)

Located at the eastern end of Great Slave Lake in the Northwest Territories, Thaidene Nëné National Park Reserve is part of a larger group of protected areas around the East Arm and Artillery Lake regions. _Thaidene Nëné_ means ‘Land of the Ancestors’ in the Dënesųłiné—or Chipewyan—language.
