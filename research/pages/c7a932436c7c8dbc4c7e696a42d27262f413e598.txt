# Babylon Wildfire on Manti-La Sal National Forest

## Closure Order in Effect for Bears Ears National Monument and Dark Canyon Wilderness, Monticello Ranger District

**Release Date:** June 28, 2026

**Contact Information:** Holly Shyrer    [4352992977](tel:4352992977) [holly.shyrer@usda.gov](mailto:holly.shyrer@usda.gov)

**Price, UT, June 28, 2026—** The Manti‑La Sal National Forest has issued [Closure Order #04‑10‑26‑05‑01](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/manti-lasal/alerts/babylon-wildfire-closure-order) to protect public health and safety during active wildfire operations on the Babylon Fire. The closure is effective June 28, 2026, at 12:00 p.m. and will remain in place through August 31, 2026, unless rescinded.

The order closes all National Forest System lands, roads, and trails within the Dark Canyon Wilderness and portions of the Bears Ears National Monument located in San Juan County on the Monticello Ranger District. This includes all roads and trails inside the described boundary.

The public is prohibited from entering the affected area which includes driving on closed Forest roads, or using trails within the closure boundary. A map of the closure area and the full order are available at: [https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/manti-lasal/alerts](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/manti-lasal/alerts)

**At-A-Glance Babylon Wildfire Update**

Over the last 24 hours, the Babylon Fire, located one mile Northeast of Babylon Pasture showed **wind‑driven growth to the Northeast, growing from 300 acres to 16,171 acres with 0% containment**. Due to quickly changing needs of this wildfire, Great Basin Complex Incident Management Team 2 has been ordered to support suppression efforts. Current resources are providing full suppression strategy to protect structures, infrastructure and other values while keeping firefighters and the public safe. Aerial support has focused on applying retardant to predicted progression areas when conditions allow, while ground crews continue scouting and developing suppression strategies and tactics. In the coming days, windy conditions will persist and temperatures are expected to stay in the upper 80s with very low humidity, which is likely to keep fire activity high.

Currently, no evacuations are in place,however, the public is encouraged to stay informed through local officials concerning evacuation orders.

Stay informed about fire events through the forest website, social media channels, Utah Fire Info, and [**InciWeb**](https://inciweb.wildfire.gov/) **, the interagency incident information system where future updates will be posted concerning this wildfire**.

**About the Forest Service:** The Forest Service has, for more than 100 years, brought people and communities together to answer the call of conservation. Grounded in world-class science and technology — and rooted in communities — the Forest Service connects people to nature and to each other. The Forest Service cares for shared natural resources in ways that promote lasting economic, ecological, and social vitality. The agency manages 193 million acres of public land, provides assistance to state and private landowners, and maintains the largest wildland fire and forestry research organizations in the world. The Forest Service also has either a direct or indirect role in stewardship of about 900 million forested acres within the U.S., of which over 130 million acres are urban forests where most Americans live.
