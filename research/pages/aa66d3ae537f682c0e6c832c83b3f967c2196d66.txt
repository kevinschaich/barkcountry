Get out there with your best (dog) friend!
5 tips to enjoy the forest with your canine companion
Hannah Hamilton
Office of Communication
October 2, 2026
A hiker, Melissa Nicolli, with her dog Cedar, on the Cottonwood Lake trail in the Custer Gallatin National Forest. (Forest Service photo by Jabus Smith)
Not sure if you can bring your dog to the national forests on your next adventure? The answer is, “Yes!” Here are a few tips and guidelines to follow during your visit with your pup. Hiking, walking, camping, swimming or paddleboarding with your furry friend can be an extra fun way to experience America’s big backyard together.
Know before you go
To really enjoy your visit, brush up on these best practices and rules before you head out to trails, campsites and other areas of the national forests. Because no one wants to keep Fido, Bella or Bark Twain safe more than you.
Be prepared
Download the National Forests & Grasslands and National Forest and Grasslands Passport apps before you head out as cell service can often be spotty in forests (from Apple store for iOS and Google Play for Android). Use the National Forests & Grasslands app to learn the trail and recreation site conditions, where amenities are located, and much more. When you get to your forest take the Check-In-Challenge using the National Forest and Grasslands Passport app, get a badge and share a selfie of you and your pup to document the day.
And remember, some trails may be hazardous for dogs, look out for sharp rocks, slippery surfaces and plants like foxtails that can penetrate skin and ears. So, definitely know before you go.
Do a healthy pet check
Before you head out, be sure your pet’s vaccinations are up to date. Same for flea and tick prevention. Then double check that identification tags have your current contact information listed, in case you get separated. Many dog owners even go so far as to use GPS pet trackers on dog collars.
After your hike, take a moment to scan your fuzzy friend. Dislodge any uncomfortable burrs stuck in their fur or bugs hitching a ride.
Consider your dog’s needs
We love to take our four-legged friends on our adventures. And lots of dogs just love to go. Before you go, think needs before exploring the forests and grasslands.
Planning a swim, hike or leisurely walk in the woods? It is essential to prepare for weather and the activities you’ll be doing. That might mean packing gear for your best friend, too. Before you go, consider the length and difficulty of the trail. The longer and more rugged it is, the more likely your pup may need protective gear for their paws.
Adventure buddies on the North Umpqua River in the Umpqua National Forest in southwestern Oregon. (Forest Service photo)
Be prepared—just like you, your pet will have needs on the trail. Bring disposable bags so you’re ready to scoop and bag whatever “surprises” your four-legged friend leaves behind. Follow Leave No Trace principles to help protect fresh water sources and keep the trial clean and enjoyable for other hikers.
Bring water and snacks for two
Enjoying the trails can be thirsty work. Avoid dehydration and overheating. Take your time and enjoy the adventure—but don’t forget to bring water for Fido. A day on the trails may also require snacks. So, remember healthy snacks for your pup, too.
Keep your dog safe … bring a leash
A hiker and a leashed dog walk along a rocky summit overlooking Lake Pend Oreille and the Clark Fork River delta. Keeping dogs leashed helps protect wildlife, including mountain goats. (Forest Service photo by Patrick Lair)
With safety in mind, follow national forest guidelines requiring dogs always be on a 6-foot leash in areas improved for recreation like campgrounds and picnic areas, as well as on interpretive trails. This keeps your best friend close but with enough slack to sniff all the good smells along the way and avoid wildlife encounters. Remember, you are responsible for your pet’s safety. Unleashed pets are more likely to startle or provoke wildlife, resulting in injury.
Fishing on the banks of the Chattooga River on the Sumter National Forest, Andrew Pickens Ranger District, April 16, 2023. (Forest Service photo by Preston Keres)
Check the forest website before your visit to see if other areas require your pet to be on leash. And if you plan to camp out under the stars, cozy up with Fido in your camper or tent.
Everything ready to go? Grab your gear, leash up your pup and head out for your next adventure, because we’re committed to keeping national forests open, safe and welcoming for all — including your dog.
A dog fetches a stick out of Wade Lake in the Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest.Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest is the largest of the national forests in Montana, covering 3.35 million acres and throughout eight Southwest Montana counties. (Forest Service photo by Preston Keres)
