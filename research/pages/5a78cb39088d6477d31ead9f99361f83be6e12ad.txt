# Winter wildlife restrictions begin Dec. 1 on the Bridger-Teton National Forest

## Dog leash regulations apply

**Release Date:** November 25, 2025

**JACKSON, Wyo., Nov. 25, 2025**— The Bridger-Teton National Forest reminds visitors that beginning Dec. 1, [Winter Wildlife Restrictions](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/alerts/winter-travel-restrictions-jackson-blackrock-big-piney-pinedale-ranger) will go into effect on the Jackson and Blackrock ranger districts to protect the south-facing slopes and low elevation habitat crucial for wildlife to survive winter.

December 1 also marks the transition from wheeled motor vehicle use to over-snow vehicle use and leash requirements for dogs in select areas.

Boundary and trailhead signage will be posted to provide information about how to enjoy winter recreation safely and responsibly. Free winter travel maps are available at district offices, on the Forest [Visitor Maps and Guides webpage](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/maps-guides), and by downloading the free Avenza Maps app.

**Winter Closure Areas**

Designated winter closure areas ( [see map](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/105176)) are essential for wildlife survival. When people or their dogs enter closed winter range, wildlife is forced to flee into a less suitable habitat. This retreat requires animals, such as deer, elk, moose, and bighorn sheep to use valuable energy they cannot spare as they deal with cold temperatures, extreme snowpack, and limited forage.

Around Jackson, some key areas closed to all human presence to protect wildlife include Josie’s Ridge and slopes above Highway 89 south to Game Creek, slopes above Cache Creek and Nelson Drive, slopes above Hoback Canyon, and slopes north of the Gros Ventre Road. The winter wildlife range closure also includes A&W Trail between Adams and Wilson canyons south of Jackson. A&W Trail crosses three different land ownerships – Teton County, National Forest, and the Jackson Hole Land Trust. To provide consistency across the entire trail and recognize the importance of big game winter range in the area, the existing closure on the JH Land Trust and Teton County owned parcels was expanded to encompass the entire trail, beginning last winter. As an alternative, Teton County provides winter access to the nearby plowed multi-use pathway along South Highway 89.

**Dog Control**

Dog control is especially important during winter since deer, elk, moose, and bighorn sheep view dogs as predators, causing them to run and expend energy critical for survival. Dog control is equally important to keep your pet safe as there have been many unfortunate incidents of dogs being hit by vehicles or kicked and injured by wildlife. The [Dog Leash Order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/alerts/dog-leash-order-jackson-ranger-district) goes into effect Dec. 1, and dogs are required to be leashed at listed developed trailheads. This applies to Cache Creek, Trail Creek, and Teton Pass where the combination of congestion and vehicles necessitates greater control. In addition, from Dec.1 to May 1, dogs are required to be leashed in all areas adjacent to critical wildlife winter range, including the Woods Canyon and Putt-Putt trails, and the Game Creek drainage from the highway to a point 1.2 miles above the gate. Off-leash dog areas are still available in many other locations including Nelson Knoll Trail.

**Motor Vehicle Travel**

Most Forest roads close to wheeled motor vehicle travel Dec. 1, except for short spurs accessing winter trailheads. This protects the road surface and allows snow to build up for grooming and over-snow vehicle use. Exceptions to the wheeled vehicle closure include Spread Creek Road on Blackrock Ranger District and Fall Creek Road on Jackson Ranger District, which remain open until Dec. 15 each year. These roads are not plowed or maintained, so checking current conditions is advised to avoid getting stuck and stranded.

**Food Storage**

Visitors are also reminded that the [Food Storage Order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/alerts/food-storage-blackrock-jackson-pinedale-greys-river-big-piney-ranger) is enforced through Jan. 15, annually, on specific areas of Jackson and Blackrock ranger districts. The regulation is in place to alleviate possible negative interactions between humans and bears. All food and any attractants with an odor that might invite the curiosity of bears to an area must be securely stored; making sure it is unavailable to bears any time the area is left unattended. Attended attractants means that a person is physically present within 100 feet and in direct sight of the food or carcass.
