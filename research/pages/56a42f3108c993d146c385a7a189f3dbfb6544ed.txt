### Can I camp anywhere on national forests?

You can generally camp anywhere on national forests, unless posted otherwise. There may be some restrictions on where your vehicle can go. No driving or camping where you will cause resource damage, such creating vehicle ruts off roadways or damaging trees or streams.

We recommend camping at least 150 feet from all lakes and streams. The distance you may pull your vehicle off an established roadway will vary, depending on individual forest policy. Generally, you may not drive more than 300 feet off a roadway to park.

[**Contact the local office**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) near where you want to go and ask about current fire restrictions.

[**Dispersed Camping Tips**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/recreation/dispersed-camping)

### Do I need reservations to camp on national forests in California?

Many campgrounds are _first-come, first-serve_.

Contact Recreation.gov for any Forest Service campground reservation at 877-444-6777 or visit [Recreation.gov](http://www.recreation.gov/) for prices and availability. You need to make reservations at least five days in advance. It can reserve individual sites 240 days in advance and group sites 360 days in advance.

If you prefer solitude and privacy, dispersed or backcountry camping may be allowed within the forest boundary. [**Contact the local forest office**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) for restrictions, such as fire restrictions that may apply.

### Do you have a list of campgrounds we can reserve?

Contact [the Forest Service office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) in the area you plan to camp. Many campgrounds on California's national forests can be reserved at [Recreation.gov](http://www.recreation.gov/) or call 877-444-6777.

### How do I make reservation to stay in Forest Service cabins or lookout towers?

Forest Service lodging sources include unique cabin and lookout tower rentals. To find out if a forest has a rental cabin or lookout, contact the [local Forest office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

### Can I bring my dog to the forest? Can I have my dog off leash?

In many Wilderness Areas, developed campgrounds, picnic areas and day use areas, dogs must be on a leash. Most other areas within national forests do not require your dog to be on a leash, but they should be under your control at all times.

We recommend keeping your dog on a leash when you around other forest users, other dogs, or  in bear country. Some Wilderness Areas do not allow dogs, so be sure to contact the [local Forest office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

_Dogs are NOT allowed to chase game animals._

### Where can I ride and/or camp with my horse on national forests?

Horses are allowed anywhere on national forests unless posted otherwise.

- You do not have to ride your horse only on established trails and roads; you can ride anywhere.
- You may take horses into Wilderness areas; however, certain trails and trailheads may not be well suited to horse use.
- Please do not tie horses to trees for long periods; use hobbles or high-lines instead.
- Avoid wet, muddy trails to minimize damage from horse hoofs.

_Horses are NOT allowed in developed campgrounds, unless specifically established for equestrian use._

### Where can I drive my Off-Highway Vehicle (OHV)? Are there OHV maps available?

In most areas of national forest, you need to stay on roads and trails that are open to motorized travel.

Do not take your ATV behind a closed gate, travel off the road or trail (cross-country), unless the area is specifically designated for that use. Motorized vehicles are not allowed in any Wilderness Area

Contact the [local forest office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) for more details and maps to help you learn where you may ride.

[Motor Vehicle Use Maps](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/data-tools/interactive-maps/mvum-finder)

### Where can I get information on hunting?

Hunting seasons, bag limits, and licenses are handled by each state's game and fish agency.

In California, that is the California Department of Fish and Wildlife. More at [www.wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Hunting](https://www.wildlife.ca.gov/Licensing/Hunting) or call (916) 928-5805.

### What are special-use authorizations?

A special-use authorization is a legal permit, lease or easement that allows occupancy, use, rights or privileges of National Forest System land. The authorization is granted for a specific use of the land for a specific period of time.

[Special use implementation](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/permits/special-use)

### Can I collect rocks, pinecones, pine boughs, logs, mushrooms...?

- Some forests have “incidental use” guidelines, and do not require a permit for some small quantities of some products.
- Other forests require a permit to remove any minerals or wood products from the national forest.
- Check with your [nearest National Forest Service office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

### Do I need a Wilderness permit?

A Wilderness Visitor's Permit is often required for overnight visits in designated wilderness areas. Some popular Wilderness Areas also require a permit for day use. For some popular Wilderness Areas permits are limited and reservations are required.

- Only one permit is required for trips which are continuous and pass through more than one Wilderness.
- One permit is required per trip per group.
- Your permit doubles as a campfire permit while in the Wilderness.

Obtain a wilderness permit at the local ranger station or [Supervisor's Office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).  It's a good idea to call for current conditions.

### If I have an emergency on the national forest, who do I contact?

While cell service can be limited on national forests, please call 9-1-1 to dispatch the nearest help.

### What is a closure on a forest?

A closure is a restriction upon certain activities or public use. For example, closures might be implemented to help prevent human-caused fires or protect human life and property.

Vehicles may be restricted on certain roads when they are wet, to prevent damage to the road and subsequent damage to soils or streams from run off. To find out about closures, contact the [local forest office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) or look for alerts on individual forest websites.

### Why do fire restrictions matter to me?

Fire restrictions are issued by the Forest Supervisor or Regional Forester after coordinating with District Rangers and Fire Management Officers on local conditions. Conditions that could warrant the issuance of fire restrictions could include high temperatures, low humidity, low fuel moistures within forest fuels, and an increased fire starts.

When in effect, fire restrictions mean campfires, stove fires, and smoking are not permitted in the restricted area.

- Charcoal, wood and coal stoves outside of dwellings are classified as campfires.
- Campfires do not include any cooking or heating device using kerosene or gasoline.
- Smoking is permitted in designated forest camp and picnic grounds or while traveling in a vehicle if an ash tray is used.

Permits authorizing campfires may be issued by designated forest officers when local conditions are favorable and/or in some Forest Service developed camp or picnic grounds. Call ahead to each [local district office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) as restrictions may vary on the forest.

### I'm interested in becoming a firefighter. How can I get more information?

To become a wildland firefighter, you must be between the 18 and 35 years old and pass a physical fitness test. Firefighters sometimes earn time and a half or "hazard duty" pay.

For more information on fire jobs, please visit [**Careers in Firefighting**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/working-with-us/careers/fire-hire).

### How can I protect my home from wildland fires?

If a fire does occur near a home in the wildlands, homeowners should create defensible space, so firefighters can help protect their homes.

Examples of defensible space and home hardening:

- Cleaning roof surfaces and gutters regularly to avoid accumulation of flammable materials.
- Removing portions of any tree extending within 10 feet of the flue opening of any stove or chimney.
- Maintaining a fuel break around all structures.
- Plus, roof and exterior of homes should be constructed of non-combustible or fire-resistant materials for roofing such as tile, slate, sheet iron, aluminum, brick, or stone.
- Wood siding, cedar shakes, exterior wood paneling, and other highly combustible materials should be treated with fire retardant chemicals.

### Does the Forest Service give away free trees?

The Forest Service no longer gives away free trees. Check with [CAL FIRE](https://www.fire.ca.gov/) for more information and grants.

### How do I sell products and services to the Forest Service?

If you sell a product or service, please see the [Contracting Opportunities](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/working-with-us/contracting) on our website.

### How do I get Smokey Bear or a Forest Service fire truck at my event?

Contact the local forest and ask to speak to the Fire Prevention Officer. Smokey Bear can only be used in Fire Prevention programs.

### Can I fly a drone on National Forest System lands?

The general answer is yes, but you cannot fly everywhere or all the time. An easy reminder... do not fly over wildfires, wildlife, or wilderness.

- Drones or unmanned aircraft system (UAS) are considered “motorized equipment” and “mechanical transport.” They cannot take off from, land in, or operate from Wilderness Areas.
- Stay clear of and do not interfere with manned aircraft operations.
- Fly your UAS at least 5 miles from an airport or backcountry airstrip.
- UAS are not allowed in areas with Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFR) such as wildfires. Search the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) site for [current TFRs](https://tfr.faa.gov/tfr2/list.html).
- Do not fly over or near wildlife. Intentional disturbance of animals during breeding, nesting, rearing of young, or other critical life history functions is not allowed unless approved as research or management.
- Keep your UAS away from populated and noise-sensitive areas, such as campgrounds, trailheads and visitor centers.
- Keep your UAS within your visual line of sight at all times.
- Take lessons and learn to operate your UAS safely.
- Lastly, please obey all privacy laws.

For more information, please visit: [www.fs.usda.gov/visit/know-before-you-go/recreational-drone-tips](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/know-before-you-go/recreational-drone-tips)

### I have trees on my property I want to removed. Can you help?

The Forest Service does not remove trees or other debris from private individual’s properties.

- Contact the Public Works Department in your city or county or the [Natural Resource Conservation Service](http://www.nrcs.usda.gov/wps/portal/nrcs/main/national/contact/).
- If you have cabin lease on Forest Service property, please [contact the closest Forest office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices) for any concerns.

### How can I receive Forest Service educational materials for my classroom?

Visit our helpful [educator website](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/educational/educators) or contact the [closest Forest Service office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

### Do I need a permit to conduct a soil sample on National Forest System lands?

It depends.

- Are you investigating a contaminated site like an underground mine or a burned, special use permitted structure?
- Are you doing this as a research project or for commercial purposes?
- Are you sampling for a private client or for a regulatory agency?

The answers determine the type of authorization. Contact the [closest Forest Service office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

### How do I get a campfire permit?

First, check current fire restrictions for the area you're planning to visit. See if a campfire, charcoal fire, or gas stove/appliance is allowed. Contact the [closest Forest Service office](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/offices).

In California, campfire permits are required for the use of campfires, charcoal fires or portable gas stoves outside of designated recreation sites when allowed. These are free and are available at all Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management, or CAL FIRE offices, or visit the [online campfire permit](https://permit.preventwildfiresca.org/) (printer required).

### How do I get a day use pass/recreation permit/Adventure Pass?

There are a few different kinds of recreation permits

- **National Recreation Pass:** National passes cover entrance fees at federal recreation area such as national parks and wildlife refuges. They also cover the use of facilities and services at many day use sites on Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management lands. These do not cover campground reservations or wilderness permit reservations. [**More information on National Recreation Passes**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/passes-permits/recreation-fees-passes) **.**
- **Adventure Pass:** Forest visitors parked in standard amenity recreation fee sites in the four southern California national forests must display a valid National Recreation or Adventure pass. This includes on the Angeles, Cleveland, Los Padres and San Bernardino national forests. These do not cover campground reservations n=or wilderness permit reservations. [**More information on Adventure Passes**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/passes/adventure-pass).

### How can someone file an arson claim for a fire that damaged property?

Since National Forest System land is generally in unincorporated areas of a county, not in a city or town with a police department, our law enforcement agents generally work with the county sheriff’s office on local criminal matters. We recommend contacting your local Sheriff’s Department to start any claim.

### Can you carry a gun in a national forest?

The USDA Forest Service regulations (Code of Federal Regulations or CFRs) related to firearms and their use on National Forest System lands include:

**36 CFR § 261.10**\- The following are prohibited:

(d) Discharging a firearm or any other implement capable of taking human life, causing injury, or damaging property as follows:

(1) In or within 150 yards of a residence, building, campsite, developed recreation site or occupied area.

(2) Across or on a National Forest System Road or a body of water adjacent thereto, or in any manner or place whereby any person or property is exposed to injury or damage as a result in such discharge.

(3) Into or within any cave.

**36 CFR 261.9(a)** \- Damaging any natural feature or property of the United States (includes trees).

**36 CFR 261.5(b)** \- Firing any tracer bullet or incendiary ammunition.

**36 CFR § 261.58**\- When provided by an order, the following are prohibited: (m) Discharging a firearm, air rifle, or gas gun. (Certain or all parts of the forest may be closed by special order to shooting. Most often in California this is due to fire restrictions).

**State laws do apply to National Forest System lands. A few select California state firearms laws are listed below. (CAPC = California Penal Code).  For complete information, visit** [**https://oag.ca.gov/firearms**](https://oag.ca.gov/firearms)

**CAPC 25400: Concealed Carry.** It is illegal for any person to carry a handgun concealed upon his or her person or concealed in a vehicle without a license issued pursuant to CAPC 26150. A firearm locked in a motor vehicle’s trunk or in a locked container carried in the vehicle other than in the utility or glove compartment is not considered concealed within the meaning of the CAPC 25400; neither is a firearm carried within a locked container directly to or from a motor vehicle for any lawful purpose (CAPC 25610). The prohibition from carrying a concealed handgun does not apply to licensed hunters or fishermen while engaged in hunting or fishing, or while going to or returning from the hunting expedition (CAPC 25640). Notwithstanding this exception for hunters or fishermen, these individuals may not carry, or transport loaded firearms when going to or from the expedition. The unloaded firearms should be transported in the trunk of the vehicle or in a locked container other than the utility or glove compartment (CAPC 25610).

**CAPC 25850: Open carry of a loaded firearm in public**. It is illegal to carry a loaded firearm on one’s person or in a vehicle while in any public place, on any public street, or in any place where it is unlawful to discharge a firearm. (Note: does not apply to any person while hunting in an area where possession and hunting is otherwise lawful or while practice shooting at target ranges (CAPC 26005, 26040). Section 26055; nothing in Section 25850 shall prevent any person from having a loaded weapon, if it is otherwise lawful, at the person’s place of residence, including any temporary residence or campsite.

**CAPC 26350: Open carry of an unloaded handgun in public.** It is generally illegal for any person to carry upon his or her person or in a vehicle, an exposed and unloaded handgun while in or on:  A public place or public street in an incorporated city or city and county; or a public street in a prohibited area of an unincorporated city or city and county. Section 26388 states: section 26350 does not apply to, or affect, the open carrying of an unloaded handgun on publicly owned land, if the possession and use of a handgun is specifically permitted by the managing agency of the land and the person carrying that handgun is in lawful possession of that handgun.

**CAPC 30500‐30530: Assault Weapons.** These penal code sections define the regulations that govern the possession, manufacture, and use of assault weapons. These regulations and prohibitions apply on National Forest lands.

**CAPC 417(2): Every person who, except in self-defense, in the presence of any other person, draws or exhibits any firearm, whether loaded or unloaded, in a rude, angry, or threatening manner, or who in any manner, unlawfully uses a firearm in any fight or quarrel is punishable as follows**:

- If the violation occurs in a public place and the firearm is a pistol, revolver, or other firearm capable of being concealed upon the person, by imprisonment in a county jail for not less than three months and not more than one year, by a fine not to exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000), or by both that fine and imprisonment.
- In all cases other than that set forth in subparagraph (A), a misdemeanor, punishable by imprisonment in a county jail for not less than three months.
