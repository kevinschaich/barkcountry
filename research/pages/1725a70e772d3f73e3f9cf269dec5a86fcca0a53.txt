# Off-Highway Vehicles (OHV)

Take a ride through the Tonto, permit may be required.

## OHV Trail Riding

The new Tonto National Forest Off-Highway Vehicle Special Recreation Permit (OHV Permit) is required for motorized vehicle access in the Bulldog Canyon, The Rolls, St. Clair, and Desert Vista OHV Permit Zones. The date for the Lower Sycamore OHV Permit Zone is to be determined.

One OHV Permit covers access to all 4 OHV Permit Zones. Every motorized vehicle in a Tonto NF OHV Permit Zone (i.e., ATVs, UTVs/SxSs, dirtbikes, Jeeps, SUVs, full-size vehicles, etc.) must have at least one rider in possession of a Tonto NF OHV Permit, issued in their name as listed on their Driver’s License. Youth under 16 years of age operating an OHV in the OHV Permit Zones do not need their own permit but must be accompanied by a guardian with a Tonto NF OHV Permit. See the [**OHV Permit FAQ**](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/100298) for more details about when a motorized use permit is needed.

Permit options include a 3-day Permit for $10, a 6-month Permit for $60, or an Annual Permit for $80. Buy an OHV Permit at [Recreation.gov.](https://www.recreation.gov/activitypass/AP22844) See step-by-step instructions in [English](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/248622) or [Spanish](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/248624) for how to buy your permit.

For more information about a specific OHV Permit Zone including maps, visit the links below. Additional sites and OHV trailheads may also be listed at the bottom of this page. Be sure you’ve reviewed the [Rules and Regulations](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/101148) of the OHV areas and please recreate responsibly!

**Tread Lightly!**

Help keep these areas clean and open by recreating responsibly. Always stay on marked, designated routes and Pack-it-in Pack-it out. No designated campgrounds and limited services exist in the OHV areas on the Tonto National Forest. It is your responsibility to clean up after yourself. Be sure to:

- **Stay on designated routes.** Do not make new trails or drive on user-created routes.
- Do not harass wildlife. Riding over plants destroys wildlife habitat.
- Signs are important for travel and your safety. Do not destroy signs. Staff and volunteers spent a lot of time installing those signs.
- Avoid creating dust. Slow down and enjoy the ride!
- Keep out of closed areas. Do not trespass. They may be closed for your safety!
- Leave ranch gates as you found them, whether opened or closed. If entering a Permit Zone with gates, always close the gates behind you, no exceptions.
- Fences keep livestock confined and are important for everyone’s safety – Do not cut fences.
- Do not drive on a route smaller than the width of your vehicle. Single track routes are for single track only, etc.
- Pack it in, pack it out. Help us keep the Forest clean and enjoyable for everyone!
- **Reduce the risk of fire.** **Make sure your vehicle’s spark arrester is in good working condition.** If camping with a campfire, be sure to put the fire DEAD out and cool to the touch before leaving the area. Always check the Forest’s website or social media pages for current Fire Restrictions.
- **Be considerate of others by sharing trails.** Pull off to the side of the trail, shut off your engine if you have a louder vehicle, and let horses and hikers pass.
- Use hand signals to show passing vehicles how many vehicles are in your group. It can save lives!

For more information on a particular trail, please contact the appropriate [Ranger Station](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/tonto/offices) on the forest. Visit our [Safety and Ethics page](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r03/tonto/safety-ethics) for more outdoor safety information.

**Motorized Trails Outside OHV Permit Zones**

Only 12% of the Tonto NF's motorized routes lie within the OHV Permit Zones listed above; the other 88% of the forest's motorized trails provide an abundance of recreation opportunity for free motorized recreation (no OHV Permit required). Please remember to stay on designated routes.

**Additional Maps & Volunteer Opportunities**

For additional [maps](https://tralaz.org/trails-and-maps-2/) created by your fellow off-road enthusiasts, visit Tonto Recreation Alliance (TRAL)’s [website](https://tralaz.org/). These are not official Forest Service maps, however, TRAL partners closely with the Tonto NF to provide accurate route information with additional valuable information about the off-road experience. You can also reach out to TRAL about [volunteer opportunities](https://tralaz.org/volunteer-with-tral/) to help preserve and improve the trail systems.
