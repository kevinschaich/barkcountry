# Annual winter restrictions on the Targhee Forest

## Know the rules before you head out

**Release Date:** December 10, 2025

**Contact Information:** [r4\_c\_t\_contactus@usda.gov](mailto:r4_c_t_contactus@usda.gov)

**EASTERN IDAHO — Dec. 11, 2025** — As days grow shorter and winter recreation ramps up on the Caribou-Targhee National Forest, several roads and areas on the Targhee side have closed or will close this week to protect critical winter range.

“The forest has numerous seasonal wildlife closure areas,” said Ben Swaner, Forest Recreation Program Manager. “Although dates differ slightly between ranger districts, the purposes are similar: to protect winter range, wildlife and other natural resources.”

Lower elevations are heavily used by wintering wildlife, making habitat protection essential.

“When people enter closed winter range, animals are forced to move to new locations,” said Nate Yorgason, Forest Wildlife Program Manager. “Moving away from people requires animals such as deer, elk and moose to use energy they cannot spare, leading to weakened conditions that affect their ability to fend off disease or predators.”

Forest officials urge visitors to recreate responsibly. Snowmobiling is prohibited in designated wildlife winter ranges and Wilderness Areas. Riders should avoid damaging vegetation by operating only where adequate snow cover exists. Hikers and motorized users should also be aware that some trails and areas are now closed, even if they appear passable due to limited snowfall.

Maps are available at all Caribou-Targhee National Forest offices. Winter recreationists are encouraged to obtain a current map before traveling. Free online maps and information are available at [https://go.usa.gov/x7yjg](https://go.usa.gov/x7yjg).

The Forest Service will continue winter patrols throughout the season.

“We encourage visitors to review the current maps and check the avalanche forecast to help them make better decisions when using the National Forest,” said Zone Patrol Captain Rayce Angell. “Let someone know where you’re going and when you’ll be back. Be prepared for changing weather conditions. Most importantly, enjoy your time on the National Forest.”

**Ranger District Information**

**Palisades Ranger District (208-523-1412)**

Winter travel restrictions begin Dec. 15. Rainey Creek, areas in the Fall Creek drainage, and National Forest slopes above the South Fork of the Snake River near Heise are closed to all human presence to protect wintering wildlife. Much of the Kelly Canyon area is closed to all motorized use from Dec. 15 through April 15 each year.

**Ashton–Island Park Ranger District (208-652-7442)**

Winter travel restrictions are now in effect. The Big Bend Ridge closure runs through Jan. 1 to prevent disturbance to migrating wildlife. Most south-facing slopes along the Teton Range are also restricted. Grooming will begin once adequate snow accumulates. Groomed cross-country ski trails are closed to snowmobiles, dogs and snowshoers. Rental cabins are available for winter use.

**Dubois Ranger District (208-374-5422)**

Winter Travel Use Plans take effect Dec. 11. Winter travel maps—available [online](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/caribou-targhee/maps-guides/winter-travel-map) and at district offices—help visitors “Know Where to Go” and identify scenic trails designated for skiing, snowmobiling and other winter activities. Users should carefully review the “Winter Cross Country Use” and “Winter Designated Routes” matrices to support wildlife survival.

**Teton Basin Ranger District (208-354-2312)**

Most lower-elevation, south-facing slopes on the Teton and Big Hole ranges went into restriction on Thanksgiving Day. The gate to Teton Canyon closed Nov. 21. Dogs must be leashed at all trailheads and are prohibited on the South Valley trail system from Dec. 1 through April 1.

The Caribou-Targhee National Forest wishes everyone a safe and enjoyable holiday season.
