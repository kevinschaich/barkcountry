# Pets

| ![man holding dog watching a deer with oak trees and leaves on ground](https://www.nps.gov/chir/planyourvisit/images/IMG_20190731_112320754.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Visitor holds the collar and leash of his pet when they encounter a deer on the trail. <br>NPS<br>### Visiting with pets is limiting.<br>### If you choose to bring a pet to the park, please observe the following regulations:<br>#### Leashed or carried pets are permitted:<br>- In the Campground<br>- Paved parking lots<br>- Lower trails between the Campground and Entrance (Bonita Creek, Silver Spur Meadow, Faraway Ranch area)<br>#### Pets (including carried pets) are not permitted:<br>- In park buildings<br>- Pets are not permitted on any trails above 5,500 feet (including Massai Point Nature, Echo Canyon, Heart of the Rocks, Natural Bridge, and Sugarloaf Trails). <br>- Pets may not be carried on trails that do not permit pets.<br>#### Pets must be on a leash at all times<br>- The leash must be no longer than 6 feet.<br>#### Pets may not be tied to a a tree or object.<br>- Do not leave pets unattended: temperatures rise quickly and predators may attack pets.<br>#### Pet owners are required to pick-up after their pets (excrement and other solid waste).<br>Pet owners violating regulations may be cited and fined.<br>[36-CFR 2.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-I/part-2#2.15) provides more details concerning pets within National Park Service areas.<br>![Leashed pet on a trail through a meadow](https://www.nps.gov/chir/planyourvisit/images/Pets-Summit_2.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Pets on leashes are allowed on the lower canyon trails <br>NPS Photo / A. Huston<br>### Follow the **BARK** guidelines when visiting the park with your dog:<br>- #### Bag Your Pet's Poop<br>  <br>- #### Always Use a Leash<br>  <br>- #### Respect Wildlife<br>  <br>- #### Know Where You Can Go<br>  <br>### Why are activities with pets in the park limited?<br>Chiricahua National Park protects wildlife and animal habitats. Pets can create conditions that may disturb these protected resources.<br>- The territorial scents dogs leave behind disrupt the behavior of native animals that this park has been set aside to protect.<br>- Dogs are predators that could chase, scare, kill, and transmit diseases (distemper, parvo virus, and mange) to wild animals.<br>- Wild animals can transmit diseases, like [rabies](https://www.nps.gov/chir/planyourvisit/safety.htm#rabies), to pets (and then to humans).<br>- Where pets are allowed, leashes also protect the experience of other visitors who may be afraid, allergic, or who do not want a pet to approach them.<br>### Are there areas outside of the park where I may take my pet?<br>Pets are allowed on trails in the surrounding [Coronado National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/recmain/coronado/recreation). Leashed pets are also allowed at neighboring [Fort Bowie National Historic Site](https://www.nps.gov/fobo/index.htm). <br>Find additional tips on how to have a safe [camping](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/camping/camping-with-pets.htm) and [hiking](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/trails/hiking-with-pets.htm) experience with your pet. <br>### Are the rules the same for horse or mule?<br>No, [horse and stock animals](https://www.nps.gov/chir/planyourvisit/horseback-riding-and-stock-use.htm) are treated differently from other pets.<br>### Definition of Service Animal<br>The [Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)](https://www.ada.gov/regs2010/service_animal_qa.html) defines a "service animal" as a dog that has been rigorously trained to do work or perform tasks for the benefit of an individual with a disability.<br>Trained service animals may be allowed in buildings and on trails. Emotional support animals and animals in training must follow the rules for pets. <br>Falsely portraying a pet as a service animal is considered fraud and is subject to federal prosecution. |
