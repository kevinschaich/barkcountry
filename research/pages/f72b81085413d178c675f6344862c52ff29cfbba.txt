# Welcome to the Nebraska National Forests and Grasslands

In the Nebraska National Forests and Grasslands you can find quiet, undeveloped spaces. Here, East meets West as seen in the wildlife and plant mix. These large tracts of public land are the closest places for Midwesterners to hunt and play. Nebraska National Forests and Grasslands include the Nebraska National Forest, Samuel R. McKelvie National Forest, Buffalo Gap National Grassland, Fort Pierre National Grassland and Oglala National Grassland.

## Popular Places

![The badlands of the Buffalo Gap National Grassland covered in tall golden grass.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/nebraska/Buffalo%20Gap%20National%20Grassland%202.jpg?h=ddb1ad0c)

Wild and weird, this landscape is vast and unforgettable. The Buffalo Gap National Grassland has a rich history and the most genetically diverse population of endangered black-footed ferrets in the country.

![A group of people are walking along a trail through unique formations in Toadstool Geological Park.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/nebraska/Toadstool%20Geological%20Park.jpg?h=b5a89f81)

Noted for its unusual geologic formations and scientifically valuable fossils, Toadstool Geologic Park is a popular destination. During your hike, look for - but don't collect! - fossils that help scientists understand animal behavior 30 million years ago.

![The entrance sign to Bessey Picnic Area surrounded by trees showing fall colors.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/nebraska/Bessey%20Fall%20Picnic.gif?h=8f825fd4)

Bessey is where the largest hand-planted forest in the western hemisphere is nestled within the most intact temperate prairie on Earth. Bessey is known for its off-highway vehicle trails, hunting, camping, kayaking, and horseback riding.

![A burrowing owl turns its bright yellow eyes to the camera.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/nebraska/Burrowing%20owl%20by%20Richland%20Dam%20at%20%20Ft%20Pierre%20National%20Grassland.jpg?h=ae2e431a)

Fort Pierre National Grassland is widely known for its mixed grass prairie that provides shelter to the greater prairie chicken, the sharptail grouse, the Chinese ring-neck pheasant, and numerous migrating birds.

![The newly renovated visitor center.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r02/nebraska/image/NGVC_LobbyRenovated_April2024.jpg?h=24ae3cb4)

The National Grasslands Visitor Center introduces and invites visitors to explore the 20 National Grasslands and Midewin National Tallgrass Prairie. These public lands are restored prairie landscapes managed by the Forest Service to conserve their natural beauty, ecological diversity, and sustainable productivity for generations to come.

![Rows of new seedlings sprout in a greenhouse.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r02/nebraska/image/20240129_Vegetation_BN_Seedlings_growing_Greenhouse_closeup_Gilbert.jpg?h=19f14c2c)

The Nursery has a long-standing reputation for producing top-quality planting stock due to its deep sandy soils, abundant water and relatively long growing season. Production facilities and equipment are state-of-the-art. The Nursery is capable of producing 4.5 million bare root conifer and hardwood seedlings each year.
