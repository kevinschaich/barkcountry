# Amendment 13

Nez Perce NF LRM Plan Amendment No. 13

**Nez Perce National Forest Land and Resource Management Plan Amendment No. 13**

**April, 1991**

Forest Plan, Chapter II, page II-26

Insert the following standard immediately before the standards for the Gospel-Hump Multipurpose Resource Development Area:

ANIMAL DAMAGE CONTROL

Cooperate with the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) in fulfilling the U.S. Department of Agriculture's responsibilities under the Animal Damage Control Act of 1931.

Forest Service animal damage control policy, to be carried out jointly with APHIS, is to initiate wildlife damage control where there is (1) a threat to public health or safety, or (2) unacceptable damage to threatened or endangered animals or plants, other wildlife, livestock or other resources, or public or private property. In coordination with APHIS and other Federal and State agencies, the Forest Service determines the need for animal damage control and approves methods, timing, and geographical areas.

Site-specific NEPA analysis and documentation will be done on all proposals for animal damage control, including gopher control and predator control. The Idaho Department of Fish and Game will be consulted, as will the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service if listed species may be present in the affected area. A need for control measures must be clearly shown and, if use of poisons or predacides is recommended, the analysis must clearly demonstrate that no other method will meet the need.

\\*\\*\\* End of Amendment \*\*\*

#### Decision Memo: Forest Plan Amendment No. 13  Nez Perce National Forest, Idaho County, Idaho

April, 1991

This amendment to the Nez Perce Forest Plan is necessary to correct the inadvertent omission of language related to the Forest's responsibilities for animal damage control.

The Animal Damage Control Act of 1931 (7 U.S.C. 426-426b) authorizes and directs the Secretary of Agriculture to "conduct such investigations, experiments, and tests as he may deem necessary in order to determine, demonstrate, and promulgate the best methods of eradication, suppression, or bringing under control on national forests...of mountain lions, wolves, coyotes, bobcats, prairie dogs, gophers, ground squirrels, jack rabbits, and other animals injurious to agriculture, horticulture, forestry, animal husbandry, wild game animals, fur-bearing animals, and birds...and to conduct campaigns for the destruction or control of such animals."

The primary responsibility for compliance with this law rests with the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) of the Department of Agriculture. However, the Forest Service determines whether or not animal damage control is necessary to accomplish multiple-use objectives on National Forest lands and approves methods, timing, and areas of control. The Forest Service role is outlined in a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Forest Service and APHIS dated March 9, 1988.

This MOU is contained in the Forest Service Manual at Chapter 1543. Further direction is set out in Chapter 2650 of the Manual. In summary, Forest Service policy is to "initiate wildlife damage control projects, when wildlife (1) threaten public health or safety, or (2) cause or threaten to cause unacceptable damage to threatened or endangered animals or plants, other wildlife, livestock or other resources, or public and private property \[FSM 2650.3(1)\]. A full range of integrated prevention or control alternatives is to be considered, and licensed hunting, fishing, and trapping are to be encouraged where practical \[FSM 2650.3(2)\]. Control activities are to be directed specifically at offending populations or individuals, and when control is necessary, offending animals are to be removed as efficiently and humanely as possible \[FSM 2650.3(3)\]. Control projects are to be carried out in accordance with methods recommended by APHIS and the involved State agencies \[FSM 2650.3(4)\]. Poison baits or cyanide guns are not acceptable within classified wilderness, and the Regional Forester approves all predator control programs within wilderness \[FSM 2323.3c)\].

In addition, Region One Supplement 54 to Chapter 2650 of the Forest Service Manual specifies that the Regional Forester shall approve all animal damage control methods that require using any amount of poisons or other chemical toxicants, that the Idaho Department of Fish and Game will be consulted, that

the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service will also be consulted if threatened, endangered, or sensitive species are known or suspected to be present, and that no animal damage control measures are permitted in areas of human concentration such as campgrounds unless an animal or animals become a threat to human health and safety.

As previously stated, the Nez Perce National Forest Plan presently makes no mention of animal damage control or the Forest's responsibilities in this area. However, Section 6 (i) of the National Forest Management Act requires that "resource plans and permits, contracts, and other instruments for the use and occupancy of National Forest System lands shall be consistent with the land management plans." Therefore, addition of the programmatic language in Amendment Number 13 is necessary to insure that any animal damage control project proposed is consistent with the Forest Plan. It should be noted that the amendment does not authorize any specific projects. Site-specific NEPA analysis and documentation are done at the project level.

Since adoption of the amendment will not significantly alter Forest Plan standards and guidelines or multiple-use goals and objectives and because the amendment reflects legal requirements and current Forest Service Manual direction, I have determined the amendment to be a nonsignificant amendment under 36 CFR 219.10(f). In addition, since the language of the amendment is programmatic and further analysis must be done at the project level, I have categorically excluded the amendment from further NEPA documentation at the Forest Plan level under 40 CFR 1508.4.

This decision to amend the Forest Plan is appealable under 36 CFR 217.3(a)(1) and (b)(1). However, policies and procedures issued in the Forest Service Manual are not appealable under 36 CFR Part 217 \[36 CFR 217.4(a)(12)\]. Appeals must be filed within 45 days after publication of legal notice in the ?Idaho County Free Press.? Notices of Appeal must meet the requirements of 36 CFR 217.9.

Additional information can be obtained from Joe Bednorz, Nez Perce National Forest, Route 2, Box 475, Grangeville, Idaho 83530.

Reply to: 1920, Forest Plan Amendment, Animal Damage Control

Date: February 1, 1991

TO: Joe Bednorz, Planning Staff

On February 1, Bruce Anderson (Salmon River District Range Con), Dick Artley (Acting TRM Staff), Spike Thompson (Forest Range Con), Phil Jahn (FWWS Staff), Steve Blair (Wildlife Biologist), and I met to discuss an animal damage control proposal on the Salmon River District and its relationship to Forest Plan consistency.

Earlier it was discovered that the Forest Plan is silent on animal damage control making it difficult to demonstrate Forest Plan consistency for project level planning.

We identified the need to amend the Plan before proceding with any project level decisions relative to animal damage control. Bruce shared with us excerpts from existing statutes (Animal Damage Control Act of 1931), a MOU between the FS and APHIS, and FSM policy regarding animal damage control. It was obvious that our responsibilities and any programmatic direction could be found in existing laws and our own directives system but just wasn't echoed in the Forest Plan.

Given this, we agreed to have Pete draft a Forest Plan amendment incorporating existing legal requirements and FSM directives. The amendment would add the programmatic direction needed in the Forest Plan and facilitate consistency findings during project level planning. We acknowledge that this amendment would only add permissive language to the Forest Plan and would not prescribe any treatments. Any ADC action would have to occur after site-specific analysis had been completed following NEPA procedures.

In terms of NFMA significance, this amendment would be nonsignificant. No long term goals or objectives are being changed. We would simply be incorporating existing laws and FS policy.

In terms of NEPA significance, we can probably categorically exclude this from further documentation since no effects will result from the amendment. Any effects analysis will be at the project level when ADC proposals are made. Interim Directive No. 18 (FSM 1952.2) allows CEs beyond the established categories found in FSH 1909.15 sec. 26. Our scoping summarized above illustrates that we would be simply incorporating existing law and FSM policy into the Plan.

Once the amendment is drafted we should route it to the Leadership Team for review.

CERTIFIED MAIL-RETURN RECEIPT REQUESTED

Dear Mr. Medberry:

This letter is my decision on your May 3, 1991, Notice of Appeal (NOA) of the Acting Nezperce National Forest Supervisor's decision regarding the Nezperce National Forest Land and Resource Management Plan (LRMP), Amendment 13.

My review was conducted pursuant to, and in accordance with, 36 CFR 217. My responsibility as reviewing officer is to ensure that the analysis and the decision are in compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and orders.

I. DECISION UNDER REVIEW

On April 16, 1991, the Acting Nezperce Forest Supervisor issued a Decision Notice (DN) adopting Amendment 13 to the Nezperce LRMP. The Amendment was issued to make the Nezperce LRMP consistent with existing laws and Forest Service Manual direction for animal damage control programs.

II. OVERVIEW OF ISSUES AND RELIEF REQUESTED

The central issue outlined in your NOA is whether Amendment 13 violates the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) by not issuing an Environmental Assessment (EA) and not adequately analyzing cumulative effects. You request that we prepare an EA to determine if effects will be significant.

III. REVIEW FINDINGS

ISSUE 1. WHETHER THE DECISION VIOLATES NEPA AND ITS IMPLEMENTING REGULATIONS.

Contention: You contend that a categorical exclusion is improper and the amendment should be considered significant, therefore, requiring full NEPA documentation. You have asked for preparation of an EA in order to determine whether an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) is necessary.

Response: The decision made by the Acting Forest Supervisor is to "Cooperate with the Animal and Plant Inspection Service (APHIS) in fulfilling the USDA's?responsibilities under the Animal Damage Control Act of 1931." Such cooperation is required by FSM 1543.14. Consequently, this amendment is not a new decision and has no new effects beyond those already disclosed in the programmatic LRMP EIS. An EA is not necessary to determine that an EIS is unnecessary.

The Forest Supervisor may, at his discretion, add language to the Forest LRMP that he feels will assist in Forest LRMP implementation. An EIS is required only if a change in the Forest LRMP is significant (36 CFR 219.10(f)). Because this amendment does not change Forest Plan direction, I agree with the Forest Supervisor's assessment that the amendment is not significant. In such cases, he must satisfactorily complete NEPA procedures (36 CFR 219.10(f)). Based on the absence of any environmental impacts, I find that the Acting Forest Supervisor has, in fact, done more than required by NEPA.

Conclusion: Amendment 13 does not constitute a major federal action that would significantly affect the quality of the human environment, nor is it a significant amendment to the Forest Plan. Therefore, an EIS is not required.

Contention: You contend that the Forest did not adequately analyze the likely cumulative effects of the many projects which will be undertaken as a result of this amendment.

Response: Cumulative effects of reasonably foreseeable future actions must be added to the "incremental impact" of the proposed action (40 CFR 1508.7). Here there is no such impact, and therefore, no cumulative effects to address.

Implementation of this Amendment will not change the land allocation contained in the Nezperce Forest LRMP, nor will it change the estimated annual outputs of the Forest LRMP. The environmental effects associated with land allocation and estimated annual outputs are disclosed in the Forest LRMP Final Environmental Impact Statement. Because no changes are made in land allocations or estimates of annual outputs, there are no environmental effects associated with the Amendment.

Moreover, I do not agree with your description of the cumulative effects that should be considered. Your NOA raises concerns about future actions that are not reasonably foreseeable: possible use of "certain control methods (such as M-44's)," and use of methods "regularly employed." NEPA does not require the Forest Service to undertake such speculation.

You have asked that the Forest look at "the likely cumulative effects of the many projects which will be undertaken." However, there is only one such project now being planned for which any assessment of effects could reasonably be made. Site-specific analysis of those effects is being performed for that project and is designed to meet NEPA requirements for site-specific analysis.

You expressed a particular concern about "sight-unseen approval" of activities by the Forest Service. The requirement of site-specific analysis for all animal damage control proposals ensures that the Forest Service will take a hard look at environmental impacts before approving such activities.

Conclusion: There are no cumulative effects of this decision to be addressed. Therefore, NEPA disclosure requirements have been met.??IV.? ??DECISION??

I affirm the Acting Nezperce Forest Supervisor's decision to proceed with Amendment 13 to the Nezperce National Forest LRMP. The decision is reasoned and in conformance with applicable laws and regulations. Your request for relief is denied.

This is the final determination of the Department of Agriculture, unless the Chief, on his own initiative, elects to review the decision within 15 days of receipt (36 CFR 217.d).

Reply to: 1570, Appeal No. 91-01-00-0086, Forest Plan Amendment #13, Idaho Conservation League

Date: May 23, 1991

To: Reviewing Officer, Northern Region

Nez Perce Forest Plan Amendment Number 13 is a programmatic amendment dealing with animal damage control programs on the Forest. The amendment does two things: it makes the Forest Plan consistent with existing laws and Forest Service Manual direction, and it establishes that site-specific NEPA analysis and documentation will be done at the project level. The amendment also specifies that a need for control measures must be clearly shown and that if use of poisons or predacides is recommended, the analysis must clearly demonstrate that no other method will meet the need.

Acting Nez Perce Forest Supervisor David E. Poncin signed the Decision Memo on Forest Plan Amendment Number 13 on April 16, 1991. On May 3, 1991 the Idaho Conservation League appealed the amendment on the ground that it does not comply with NEPA and requested that an Environmental Assessment be prepared to determine whether the amendment is significant.

CONTENTION

A NEPA determination of significance must be done at the Forest Plan level for Forest Plan Amendment Number 13.

All of the language in Forest Plan Amendment 13 is either taken directly from or is based upon Forest Service Manual direction and the requirements of NEPA. The Manual citations are included in the Decision Memo. As stated in the Decision Memo, Manual direction is not appealable under 36 CFR Part 217 \[36 CFR 217.4 (a)(12)\]. For this reason, public notification that the Plan was being amended \[36 CFR 219.10(f)\] instead of a Decision Memo may have sufficed. However, in view of the sensitivity of the subject matter, the Nez Perce Forest decided to provide for administrative review by issuing a Decision Memo.

The appellant's concern is with environmental effects. The Decision Memo states that "the amendment does not authorize any specific projects. Site-specific NEPA analysis and documentation are done at the project level." Neither does the amendment propose any projects. Since the scope of future proposals (40 CFR 1508.25) and the contexts and intensities of effects (40 CFR 1508.27) are not specified, site-specific analysis of the significance of effects cannot be done except through site-specific project-level analysis which tiers to the Forest Plan direction.

Several ranger districts are currently analyzing the continued use of poison to control gopher populations in tree plantations, and the Salmon River District is analyzing a predator control project proposed by APHIS. These are the only animal damage control proposals currently being considered on the Nez Perce Forest, and any decision documents on them will discuss NEPA significance. As a result of the Decision Memo on Forest Plan Amendment Number 13, the Nez Perce Forest has been alerted to Mr. Medberry's concerns about animal damage control; and he has been invited to participate in the project-level analysis.
