# Anaconda Pintler Wilderness-Specific Regulations

## Keep the Wild in Wilderness

Visiting the Anaconda Pintler Wilderness comes with a shared responsibility to protect its wilderness character. To keep the area healthy and wild, managers use education and information to guide visitors and reduce impacts. When rules are needed, they’re designed to protect the wilderness while still allowing people to explore and enjoy it. [Find out more about recreation in the A-P](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r01/beaverhead-deerlodge/recreation/anaconda-pintler-wilderness/).

[Anaconda Pintler Wilderness Management Direction Map](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/44053)

## General Wilderness Prohibitions (36 CFR 261.18)

- It is prohibited to use a motor vehicle, motorboat or motorized equipment except as authorized by federal law or regulation.
- It is prohibited to use a hang glider or bicycle.
- It is prohibited to land an aircraft, or drop or pick up of any materials, supplies, or person by means or aircraft including a helicopter.
- Possessing or using a wagon, cart, bicycle or other vehicle (including game carts) is prohibited.

## Permit Required in the A-P Wilderness

A self-issued permit/registration is required to recreate in the Anaconda Pintler Wilderness. 36 CFR 261.57(a)

Permit forms are located at the trailheads and are required for entry into the wilderness. Visitors can also fill out all and print the [Anaconda Pintler Wilderness Recreation Use form](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/44481) at home. Make sure to fill in all sections and leave the appropriate copy in the collection box at the trailhead. Keep the remaining copy and the list of regulations with you.

## No campfires within 1/4 mile of the shoreline at these lakes:

Oreamnos Lake, Upper Phyllis Lake, Surprise Lake, Buck Lake, Unnamed Lake below Queener Mtn., Lost Lakes, Sawed Cabin Lake, Upper Carpp Lake, Bear Lake, Emerald Lake, Lower Phyllis Lake, Continental Lake, Sauer Lake, Park Lakes, Unnamed Lake west of Warren Lake. (36 CFR 261.52(a))

## Maximum group size: 12 people, 12 head of stock

Group size within the A-P Wilderness is limited to a maximum number of 12 people and 12 head of pack and riding stock. "Stock" is defined to include horses, mules, burros, llamas and goats. (36 CFR 261.57(b) & 261.58(f))

## Traveling and camping with stock

- Maximum head of pack and riding stock is 12.
- Weed seed free feed is required for stock. [Weed Free Forage & Mulch Products Order for the Northern Region](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r01/alerts/weed-free-forage-mulch-products-order-northern-region) (36 C.F.R § 261.58(t))
- Grazing or tethering of stock within 200 feet of any lake is prohibited. (36 CFR 261.57(a) & 261.57(e))
- Camping with stock is prohibited within 1/4 mile of Oreamnos Lake, Sawed Cabin Lake, and Ripple Lake.
- Stock is prohibited within 200 feet of Big Johnson Lake except on designated trail.
- Hope Lake Trail #424 is closed to stock travel. (36 CFR 261.57(a) & 261.57(b))
- Use of stock in the Pintler Ranger District portion of the Anaconda Pintler Wilderness Area is prohibited April 1 thru July 1. (36 CFR 261.57(a) & 261.57(b))

## Camping stay limits & Equipment

- Camping in an area or campsite for more than _16 consecutive days_ within any calendar year is prohibited.
- Camping or otherwise occupying any number of sites over _32 cumulative days_ within a calendar year is prohibited.
- Storing equipment, personal property, or supplies for more than 14 consecutive days within any 5-day period. Disposing of any debris or garbage, including excess livestock salt is prohibited. This does not prohibit the destruction of combustible material by burning.

## Areas closed to camping

- Camping is prohibited between Trails #96 and #111 and the lakeshore of Johnson Lake.
- Camping with stock is prohibited within 1/4 mile of Oreamnos Lake, Sawed Cabin Lake, and Ripple Lake.

## Food storage requirements

Food storage requirements help keep both people and wildlife safe in bear country. Black and grizzly bears have a strong sense of smell and can be drawn to human food or garbage if it isn’t stored properly. When bears get into human food, they can become bolder around people, which increases the chance of dangerous encounters. Proper food storage helps prevent conflicts and keeps bears wild.
