# Lake Ann Trail 600

Beginning at Austin Pass, the Lake Ann Trail enters [Mt. Baker Wilderness](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/recreation/mt-baker-wilderness) as it switchbacks down into the headwaters of the Swift Creek Drainage. Bordered by Shuksan Arm to the north and Artist Ridge to the south, you will travel through meadows and wooded areas. Approximately 2.5 miles from the trailhead is a signed junction with [Swift Creek Trail 607](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/recreation/trails/swift-creek-trail-607).

As you begin to climb out of the Swift Creek drainage through heather, talus slopes and subalpine forest, Mt. Baker comes into view. You will reach a saddle above Lake Ann. Dropping down toward the lake, you will encounter an unsigned junction. The left fork leads to the popular Fisher Chimneys route for climbers attempting to summit Mt. Shuksan. The right fork leads to lakeside campsites.

Lake Ann hikers are rewarded with up-close views of Mt. Shuksan and its Lower and Upper Curtis Glaciers. Listen for the thunderous roar that means a chunk of ice has broken off one of the glaciers and is tumbling down the rock face.

Lake Ann Trail is part of the [Pacific Northwest Scenic Trail](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/recreation/groups/pacific-northwest-trail-recreation) system.

## Specific Trail Information

### Trail Number

600

### Trail Type

**Standard/Terra Trail** — A trail that has a surface consisting predominantly of the ground and that is designed and managed to accommodate use on that surface.

## General Information

Black bears frequent the area. Food and scented items need to be stored in a secure manner.

**Maps:**

• Green Trails: Mt. Shusksan #14

• USGS: Shuksan Arm

• USFS: Mt. Baker Ranger District and Mt. Baker Wilderness

### Seasons of Use

Late Summer - Fall

### Fee Site and Info

$5 per vehicle per day

$30 per annual

Day use fee payment options: Scan & Pay on-site or display a valid [Recreation Pass](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/passes).  _\*Suggestion: Download the_ [_recreation.gov app_](https://www.recreation.gov/mobile-app) _before your trip to have the ability to scan & pay digitally on-site._ Recreation Passes honored for day use fee payment: Digital Passes, Northwest Forest Pass, and Interagency Recreation Passes.

_Washington State Discover Pass is not valid at US Fee Sites._

### Permits

Visitors staying overnight in [North Cascades National Park](https://www.nps.gov/noca/index.htm) need a free backcountry permit available at the Glacier Public Service Center June-September. All other months contact the National Park directly at 360-854-7245, or email [noca\_wilderness@nps.gov](mailto:noca_wilderness@nps.gov).

### Water Available

No potable water.

### Restrictions

- Hikers only.
- No fires, except self-contained, carry-in stoves.
- No snowmobiles.
- Dogs must be leashed within the developed recreation area of Heather Meadows.
- On trail, make sure pets are kept on leashes no longer than 6 feet in designated recreation areas. Pets can spook horses or other pack animals, impact others' camping experiences, and more. (36 CFR 261.16J)
- Lake Ann Trail enters the [Mt. Baker Wilderness](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/recreation/mt-baker-wilderness). Wilderness regulations apply, including a maximum party size of 12.
- Fishing requires a [Washington State Fishing License](http://wdfw.wa.gov/licensing/).
- Once trail enters [North Cascades National Park](http://www.nps.gov/noca/index.htm) national park regulations apply: overnight use requires a backcountry permit, hunting and pets are prohibited.

## Getting There

### Trailhead/Starting Points

The trailhead is only accessible by vehicle when the Mt. Baker Highway SR542 is open all the way to Artist Point. When the highway is closed to Artist Point, the closest parking is at [Bagley Lakes Trailhead](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/recreation/bagley-lakes-trailhead). Winter access requires route finding and travel through avalanche terrain.

### Parking

28 parking spaces at trailhead

### Directions

Travel east to the end of Mt. Baker Highway SR 542. It is approximately 23 miles from the [Glacier Public Service Center](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mbs/offices/glacier-public-service-center) to the Lake Ann Trailhead. The parking lot is located on the left (east) side of SR 542, one mile before Artist Point.
