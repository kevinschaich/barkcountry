# Trails

|     |
| --- |
| One of the best ways to enjoy and experience Petrified Forest National Park is on foot.<br>- Stay on designated trails while in developed hiking areas. Off-trail hiking can damage the fragile grassland environment, disturb wildlife habitat, and/or create unsightly "social" trails.<br>- Pets are allowed on the developed trails. [Pets](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/pets.htm) must be kept on leash at all times. Please clean up after your animal. Be sure to get your BARK Ranger certificate!<br>- [Bicycles](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/bicycling.htm) (including ebikes) are not allowed on trails, except for the half mile access trail that leads to the junction between the Long Logs and Agate House Trails.<br>Most trails are paved and barrier free, but several have grades in excess of ADA requirements. Portions of some trails are deemed accessible. See the descriptions below for more specific information.<br>If you are looking for off-trail adventures, check out our [Hiking](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/hiking.htm) page.<br>![Hiker walks through grassland under sunset skies.](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/20170426-Tawa-Trail-Jholgerson-2-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Hiker walks through grassland under sunset skies along the Tawa Trail. <br>NPS/Jacob Holgerson<br>**Tawa Trail**<br>Length: 1.2 mile (1.9 km) one way<br>Trailheads: Tawa Point and Painted Desert Visitor Center<br>Enjoy the tranquility of the grassland as the trail leads you from scenic Tawa Point to the Painted Desert Visitor Center. The Tawa Trail is a perfect place to stretch your legs after a long road trip.<br>In Hopi ideology, Tawa refers to the Sun Spirit, the Creator of the World. The Hopi are one of several current Native American groups who are connected to the rich, varied history of Petrified Forest.<br>_Groomed surface_<br>_Elevation gain: 81 ft (24 m)_<br>_Elevation loss: 28 ft (8.5 m)_<br>_Accessibility information: Meets standards for outdoor developed areas._<br>_Typical grade 2.3% with grades up to 8.3%_<br>_Typical cross slope: 2%_<br>_Max cross slope: 5%_<br>_Typical tread width: 60 in (152.4 cm)_<br>_No obstacles_<br>![Woman enjoys the view of the red Painted Desert from the Rim Trail](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Rim-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Enjoying the Rim Trail <br>NPS<br>**Painted Desert Rim Trail**<br>Length: 1 mile (1.6 km) round trip<br>Trailheads: Tawa Point and Kachina Point<br>This unpaved trail winds through the rim woodland, a place for chance encounters of many species of plants and animals and spectacular views of the Painted Desert.<br>_Tread width: 62 in (158.6 cm), min 60 in (152.4 cm)_<br>_Grade: 6.3%, max 25.6%_<br>_Cross Slope: 3.8%, max 16.2%_<br>_This trail does not have stairs, but the waterbars and dirt-gravel surface may make this trail unsuitable for strollers or mobility vehicles._<br>![Visitors looking into the kiva at Puerco Pueblo](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Puerco-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Visitors looking into the kiva at Puerco Pueblo <br>NPS<br>**Puerco Pueblo**<br>Length: 0.3 mile (0.5 km) loop<br>Trailhead: Puerco Pueblo parking area<br>A paved walk amidst the remains of a hundred room pueblo, occupied by the ancestral Puebloan people over 600 years ago. Petroglyphs can be viewed along the south end of the trail. Please do not climb on the boulders or walls and do not touch the petroglyphs.<br>_Suitable for strollers and various mobility equipment (power and manual), except on short trail spur_<br>![Two visitors walk the trail among the badlands at Blue Mesa](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Blue-Mesa-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Walking the Blue Mesa Trail <br>NPS<br>**Blue Mesa**<br>Length: 1 mile (1.6 km) loop<br>Trailhead: Blue Mesa sun shelter<br>Descending from the mesa, this alternately paved and gravel trail loop offers the unique experience of hiking among badland hills of bluish bentonite clay as well as petrified wood. Numerous plant and animal fossils have been found by paleontologists in the sedimentary layers of Blue Mesa.<br>_Steep grade at beginning of trail_<br>![Man with dog walks the Crystal Forest Trail](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Dog-and-Dad-at-Crystal-Forest-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Exploring Crystal Forest <br>NPS<br>**Crystal Forest**<br>Length: 0.75 mile (1.2 km) loop<br>Trailhead: Crystal Forest parking area<br>Named for the presence of beautiful crystals that can be found in the petrified logs, this trail offers one of the best opportunities to experience the petrified wood deposits. Please leave the petrified wood for others to enjoy. Report any removal of petrified wood or other materials to park staff.<br>_Tread width: 71 in (179.8 cm), min 65 in (165.1 cm)_<br>_Grade: 5.2%, max 22.5%_<br>_Cross Slope: 2.2%, max 11.3%_<br>![Visitors walk through the petrified wood along Giant Logs Trail](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Giant-Logs-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Giant Logs Trail <br>NPS<br>**Giant Logs**<br>Length: 0.4 mile (0.6 km) loop<br>Location: Behind Rainbow Forest Museum<br>_Trail guide available at Rainbow Forest Museum_<br>Giant Logs features some of the largest and most colorful logs in the park. "Old Faithful", at the top of the trail, is almost ten feet wide at the base!<br>_This paved trail has several sets of stairs and may not be suitable for strollers or mobility equipment._<br>_Tread width: 57 in (145.6 cm), min 48 in (121.9 cm)_<br>_Grade: 4.8%, max 19.1%_<br>_Cross Slope: 2.5%, max 13.4%_<br>**Long Logs and Agate House Trails can be combined for a total of 2.6 miles (4.2 km) round trip.**<br>![Visitors walk among the petrified wood at Long Longs](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Long-Logs-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Long Logs Trail <br>NPS<br>**Long Logs**<br>Length: 1.6 mile (2.5 km) loop<br>Trailhead: Rainbow Forest Museum parking area<br>Long Logs is one of the largest concentrations of petrified wood in the park. Explore this ancient log jam at the base of gray badlands. Please leave petrified wood for others to enjoy. Report any removal of petrified wood or other materials to park staff.<br>_The first half-mile of this trail is paved and suitable for mobility equipment and strollers. Strollers may be negotiated on the loop, but it is not recommended for most wheelchairs and other mobility equipment due to its narrow turns and rough surface. Stairs to shade shelter can be avoided._<br>_Tread width: 85 in (216 cm), min 50 in_<br>_Grade: 3.5%, max 20.5%_<br>_Cross Slope: 2.9%, max 17.4%_<br>![Agate House is an ancient building constructed of petrified wood.](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/images/Agate-House-Trail-400.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)Agate House <br>NPS<br>**Agate House**<br>Length: 2 miles (3.2 km) round trip<br>Trailhead: Rainbow Forest Museum parking area<br>Archeologists believe that this small pueblo was occupied for a short time about 700 years ago. Agate House was an eight room home with entered through the ceiling. Please leave any archeological artifacts for all to enjoy. Do not climb or sit on site walls. Due to physical safety concerns, Agate House is closed to the public; visitors must stay a minimum of 15’ away from the structure. <br>_The first half-mile of this trail is paved and suitable for strollers and various mobility equipment (power and manual). The rest of the trail may be negotiated over rough surface._<br>_Tread width: 75 in (191.3 cm), min 60 in (152.4 cm)_<br>_Grade: 4.1%, max 14.7%_<br>_Cross Slope: 2.3%, max 10.4%_ |
