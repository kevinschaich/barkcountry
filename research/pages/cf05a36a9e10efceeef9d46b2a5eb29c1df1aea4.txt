# Progress Continues to Reopen Trails on De Soto National Forest

**Release Date:** March 4, 2021

**Wiggins, MS, March 2, 2021** – Although wet weather and flooded trail conditions have hampered efforts to access and clear portions of some trails, good progress continues to be made in reopening trails and recreation areas on the De Soto Ranger District. In the wake of Hurricane Zeta’s landfall last October, unsafe conditions such as such as debris, hazard trees, erosion, and infrastructure damage led to closure of recreation areas and trails on the Forest. Since that time, 8 developed recreation areas and dozens of miles of trails have reopened thanks to the dedication and hard work of Forest Service personnel, volunteers and volunteer groups, partners, cooperators, and contractors.

The following trails or sections of trails are now open:

- Bethel Bike Trail
- Bethel Motorcycle Trails – lower portion
- Black Creek Hiking Trail – section from Big Creek Landing to CCC Camp and the section from Melvin Breland Road to Fairley Bridge Landing
- Big Foot Horse Trail – blue, yellow, and green loops only
- Tuxachanie Trail – Hwy 49 trailhead to Airey Lake

Also open are the following:

- All Landings on Black Creek are open for day-use and river access only; no overnight camping is allowed
- Black Creek Shooting Range
- Ashe Lake Recreation Area
- Big Biloxi Recreation Area campground is open at 50% capacity to self-contained campers; no tent camping is allowed
- Tent camping is permissible in the general forest area only

Restrooms and shower facilities at all recreation areas are closed to reduce the risk of exposure to coronavirus for the public and Forest personnel. Airey Lake and POW Lake Recreation Areas remain closed due to continuing hazards to public safety. Full reopening of trails and recreation areas will occur when hazards are mitigated, and areas are deemed safe for the public to occupy.

To check the status of recreation areas or to sign-up for volunteer opportunities, contact the De Soto Ranger District at (601) 528-6160. Alternately, visit the National Forests in Mississippi website at [https://www.fs.usda.gov/mississippi/](https://www.fs.usda.gov/mississippi/) or follow us on Facebook @USFSMississippi.
