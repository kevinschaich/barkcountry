# Russian River fishery opens June 11 with Forest Order in place to protect anglers and wildlife

**Release Date:** June 9, 2026

**Contact Information:** Alex McLain    [alexander.mclain@usda.gov](mailto:alexander.mclain@usda.gov)

**Cooper Landing, AK**– The Chugach National Forest is renewing several public safety orders regulating food storage and pet leashing in anticipation of sport fishing season opening June 11. The Russian River, centrally located on the Kenai Peninsula, is a popular sockeye salmon fishery that sees approximately 150,000 visitors each summer, and is where the Chugach National Forest manages a campground, 130 day-use parking spaces, and trails connecting these sites to the Russian River and the Russian River Falls salmon viewing platform. Renewing these orders provides for the safety of anglers and wildlife alike.

“With the large number of anglers we see on the Russian River each summer, there is a very high potential for dangerous human-bear conflicts,” said Seward District Ranger Ruth D’Amico. “We are asking anglers to reduce that risk by following the rules; keeping food and fish stringers out of bears' reach and keeping all dogs on a 6-foot leash.”

This Forest Order is mirrored by regulations enforced by the Kenai National Wildlife Refuge, which manages the land on the opposite side of the Russian River accessible by the Russian River Ferry. Thus, anglers using the area only need to learn one common set of rules to be in compliance.

The Forest Service is the lead agency of the Russian River Interagency Coordination Group, which works to protect public resources in the area and consists of representatives from the State of Alaska Department of Natural Resources, Kenai National Wildlife Refuge, Streamwatch Volunteer Program, and Kenaitze Indian Tribe.

[Text of the Forest Order can be found here.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r10/chugach/alerts/russian-river-occupancy-and-use-restrictions) The Chugach National Forest asks visitors to act responsibly when visiting the Russian River:

- **Keep all bear attractants (food, beverages, garbage) as well as equipment used to cook or store food in a bear resistant-container, in your vehicle, or within 3 feet of you at all times.**
- **Keep all retained fish (i.e. stringers) within 12 feet of you at all times.**
- **Minimize fish waste available to bears by:**
- Taking fish out whole – Please take fish out whole when possible (gutting and gilling is acceptable) and manage fish waste offsite in a responsible manner based on local solid waste standards.
- Processing fish only at established fish cleaning facilities. If you clean onsite, please Stop, Chop and Throw.  STOP at fish cleaning tables at the confluence or ferry site, CHOP fish waste into numerous small pieces and THROW the pieces into fast-moving river current.
- Helping move fish waste downstream by regularly moving any fish waste you encounter along the shore or hanging up on rocks into faster moving water so it will move downstream.
- **Respect areas that are fenced or roped off to protect sensitive plants and cultural sites. Stay on established trails and boardwalks and use the public restroom facilities dispersed throughout the area.**
