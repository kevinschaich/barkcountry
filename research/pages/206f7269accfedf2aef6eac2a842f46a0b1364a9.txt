# Pilot Knob Trailhead

Pilot Knob Trailhead is located in the Northern Hills Ranger District and is often considered the "hub" of trail activity within the Black Hills National Forest. The trailhead provides access to numerous recreational resources in the area, such as the ever-popular Centennial Trail for both motorized and non-motorized trail users.

In recent years, the trailhead has become a staging point with backpackers, using the area as a starting point to explore the forest.  The trailhead is located approximately 52 miles south of Bear Butte State Park, which is home to the north end of the Centennial Trail, making the trailhead a prime site for supplying through-hikers.

Perhaps most important, the trailhead provides access to multiple motorized trails and forest service roads. OHV riders are encouraged to plan accordingly with required permits for 62-inch trails and also be prepared for increased summer season traffic. Single track and rock crawler trails are also available in the local area.

NOTE: Due to significant use during the summer season, all trail users are encouraged to consider other recreational sites on the Forest.

## General Information

### Seasons of Use

Open Year-Round

### Fee Site and Info

There are no fees associated with trailheads.

### Pet Information

Dogs in developed recreation sites, such as campgrounds, picnic sites, and day-use areas, must be on a leash no longer than 6 feet at all times. Furthermore, dogs are not allowed on any swim beaches on the Forest.  For the safety and comfort of fellow recreationalists, the Forest encourages guests to avoid leaving pets unattended at campsites and/or in parked vehicles.

## Getting There

### Directions

To access Pilot Knob Trailhead from Lead, South Dakota, travel south on US Highway 385 for approximately 22 miles. Turn left (east) onto FSR 208 (Merritt Estes Road) and travel for about one mile to the trailhead entrance.

### Parking

Please be advised that the trailhead can only accommodate a limited number of vehicles and trailers. Depending on the time of day and season, trail users may find it difficult to park at the trailhead. The Forest encourages alternate parking plans or trail routes to avoid the potential congestion.
