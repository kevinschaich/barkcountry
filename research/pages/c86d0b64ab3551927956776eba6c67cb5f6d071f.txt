# Respect Wildlife

Our national forests are home to an abundant and diverse wildlife population. As use of our public land grows and the pressure on wildlife increases, it is our responsibility to avoid stressing wildlife and to respect their habitat.

## Follow the Rule of Thumb:

Image

[![Rule of thumb for wildlife](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/medium/public/r02/whiteriver/image/Rule%20of%20thumb.jpg?itok=bNVry9Om)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r02/whiteriver/image/Rule%20of%20thumb.jpg)

Wildlife experts recommend keeping at least **100 yards away from bears and big game** and **25 yards from other animals.**

With your arm fully extended, you should be able to cover the entire animal with your thumb.

[Living with Wildlife](https://cpw.state.co.us/living-wildlife)

### Be Bear Aware

Image

[![GMR_Bear Warning_Blue River Campground_2](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/medium/public/legacy-media/whiteriver/GMR_Bear%20Warning_Blue%20River%20Campground_2.jpg?itok=adnDbkKK)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/legacy-media/whiteriver/GMR_Bear%20Warning_Blue%20River%20Campground_2.jpg)

Bears are **opportunists:** they will feed on whatever is readily available. Food odors and improperly stored garbage will attract bears to campgrounds and picnic sites. Bears become habituated to human food if it is available.

Black bears are found throughout the White River National Forest, and the potential for **bear-human interactions is high**.

Although bears are naturally afraid of humans, the animals lose this fear as they begin to associate human scents with the reward of food. Bears can become a threat to humans, property and themselves—a pattern that normally ends with death to the bear.

If you are camping in developed or dispersed campsites on the White River National Forest between **May 15 and Oct. 1**, you must **store all food** **and other items** that might attract bears where bears can't access them, such as a hard-sided vehicle or bear-resistant food container or locker. [View order here](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/alerts/food-storage-order).

[Bear-resistant food containers](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/safety-ethics/bear-resistant-containers) are also **required** when backpacking in the **Maroon Bells-Snowmass Wilderness**, and **highly recommended** in other **backcountry areas**. [View order here.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r02/whiteriver/alerts/maroon-bells-snowmass-wilderness-occupancy-and-use)

Protect yourself and protect the black bears by storing trash and food in safe locations when you visit a national forest. Visit the [**CPW Living with Wildlife**](https://cpw.state.co.us/living-wildlife) and [**How to be Bear Aware in Black Bear Country**](https://fws.gov/story/2022-06/how-be-bear-aware-black-bear-country#:~:text=1%20Make%20noise%2C%20sing%20or%20talk%20loudly.%20Always,bear%20spray%20and%20know%20how%20to%20use%20it.) for additional safety information.

### Items to Store Properly

- Fresh and canned food, soft drinks, alcoholic beverages, cooking supplies, coolers
- Garbage, food scraps, empty food and beverage containers
- Other attractants including pet food, bird feeders, cooking equipment, soap, toothpaste and deodorants

### Respect Moose

Image

[![A bull moose in an aspen grove looks straight at the camera.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/medium/public/legacy-media/whiteriver/Bull%20Moose%20at%20Maroon%20Lake%20September%2012%202012.jpg?itok=Z5YTqIec)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/legacy-media/whiteriver/Bull%20Moose%20at%20Maroon%20Lake%20September%2012%202012.jpg)

Moose, the largest members of the deer family, are found throughout the White River National Forest. Moose encounters with people are common. Most encounters are peaceful, however, moose can become aggressive.

Moose need their space. They can move quickly - up to 35 miles per hour - and must be given respect.

To avoid a conflict with a moose:

- Ensure your dog is **leashed**, the mere presence of a dog can increase the potential for conflict.
- Stay a safe distance away and do not approach. If possible, face the animal and back away slowly.
- Avoid surprising wildlife

[Living with Moose](https://cpw.state.co.us/living-moose)

### Calving Season - Leave Young Animals Alone

Image

[![Calving season](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/medium/public/r02/whiteriver/image/Leave%20Babies%20alone_0.jpg?itok=myn_zXkP)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r02/whiteriver/image/Leave%20Babies%20alone_0.jpg)

The White River National Forest is renowned for both its incredible year-round recreational opportunities as well as its tremendous wildlife resources. Maintaining both requires finding a **balance**, particularly **during sensitive times** in **sensitive calving areas.**

Elk return to these areas every year because they provide the water, forage, and seclusion needed to survive, birth, and nurse without being interrupted. Repeated disturbances to elk from people and dogs lead to lowered calf survival rates.

Young fawns and calves need to be left alone from humans and pets. If they are alone, they have NOT been abandoned. Mom is more than likely nearby watching, so it is important not only for the survival of the young but for your safety to not feed, harass or approach any young animals.

The White River National Forest has worked closely with Colorado Parks and Wildlife to **identify the most critical calving areas for elk**, which are **seasonally** **closed** **to all uses to reduce disturbance**.

It is important that you refrain from visiting these critical areas located within the **Aspen-Sopris** and **Eagle-Holy Cross ranger districts** during the seasonal trail closures to protect calving elk.

Visit [**CPWs Leave Young Wildlife Alone**](https://cpw.state.co.us/dont-rescue-young-wildlife) for more information regarding elk calving season.
