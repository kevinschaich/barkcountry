## Regulations

If you see something suspicious, do not hesitate, report it. Your information and participation
contributes to the protection of this special place for all visitors.

Contact Banff Dispatch 24/7 at [403-762-1470](tel:+1403-762-1470).


Laws are designed to protect you, the visitor, and also the Park for enjoyment by future generations. It is your
responsibility to be aware of the laws. If you have any questions please contact the [Banff Visitor Centre](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/visit/installations-facilities).

Park Wardens are Federal Peace Officers responsible for the protection of the National Park, and preservation and
maintenance of public peace. Their primary responsibility is enforcement of the _[National Parks Act](http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-14.01/index.html)_, however, they
also enforce _[Criminal Code](http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-46/)_, liquor and highway
regulations among others.

Breaking the law in a National Park can result in eviction, a ticket or in more serious instances, arrest. Canadian
National Park offences can carry heavy fines and could result in jail time or restitution. For some offences, such
as poaching or pollution, fines can be in excess of $250,000. View a complete listing of the [_Canada National Parks Act_](http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-14.01/index.html).

All _Canada National Parks Act_ offences result in automatic court appearances.

Visitors should familiarise themselves with the [_Canada National Parks Act_](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-14.01/) and the [_National Parks General_\\
_Regulations_](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/n-14.01/page-2.html).

Please contact a [Parks Canada visitor centre](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/visit/installations-facilities) for questions about the following regulations:

**Activities requiring a permit**

There are a number of outdoor activities that require a permit such as research and collection,
commercial videography and photography, and commercial guiding. Additionally, some activities are
restricted in some or all areas of the park. Restrictions are listed under Important Bulletins.

- [Aquatic Invasive Species\\
(AIS) Prevention Self-Certification Permit](https://parks.canada.ca/error404?item=web%3a%7b26DD5464-8A09-4F4F-BAAA-BA5DBD2B17D5%7d%40en)
- [Commercial Permits](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/permis-permit)
- [Research and Collection Permit System](https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/rps/page1_e.asp)
- [Important Bulletins](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins)

**Aircraft**

The Banff airstrip is available only for emergency and diversionary landings. You cannot take-off or
land an aircraft in a national park without a Restricted Activity Permit from Parks Canada, with
some exceptions as listed in the National Parks of Canada Aircraft Access Regulations. All aircraft
must comply with the Canadian Aviation Regulations and National Parks of Canada Aircraft Access
Regulations. Drones or unmanned aerial vehicles are considered aircraft by Transport Canada; all
Parks Canada places are “no drone zones” for recreational use.

- [National Parks\\
of Canada Aircraft Access Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-97-150/page-1.html)
- [Canadian\\
Aviation Regulations](https://www.tc.gc.ca/en/transport-canada/corporate/acts-regulations/regulations/sor-96-433.html)
- [Use of Unmanned Aerial\\
Vehicles (UAV) or drones in Canada’s national parks](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/permis-permit/drone)

**Alcohol**

The open consumption of alcohol in public places is not permitted in Banff National Park. Consuming
alcohol is allowed only at registered campsites, private residences or on licensed premises. Alcohol
is not allowed at beaches, day-use areas, cook shelters or on trails.

The consumption of alcohol and cannabis is prohibited from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. daily, and a
full ban on possession and consumption is in effect during summer long weekends in frontcountry
campgrounds in Banff National Park. Find the full restricted activity order under the [Important Bulletins](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins).

**Area closures and restrictions**

Area closures and restrictions are implemented to protect natural or cultural resources or for
visitor safety reasons, and are enforceable by law. Closure notices and restrictions will be posted
at trailheads, access points, park offices, visitor centres and under [Important Bulletins](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins).

**Bicycling**

Bicycles are permitted only on roads and [designated trails](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/activ/cyclisme-biking/velomontagne-mountainbiking) in Banff
National Park.

**Boating**

Lake Minnewanka is the only waterbody in Banff National Park where boats with motors, both gas and
electric, are allowed. Use of personal watercraft (e.g. Sea-Dos, Jet Skis etc.), boats with ballast
systems, tanks and/or bags including after-market additions and towed water sports (e.g. water
skiing) are not permitted on any water bodies.

Clean, drain, dry and certify all watercraft and water-related gear before entering a new waterbody
to prevent the spread of aquatic invasive species.

Any type of inflatable is considered a vessel when it is used for navigating and each occupant/passenger
requires a life vest / Personal Flotation Device and safety equipment.

Boating under the influence of alcohol or drugs is illegal.

For all motorized watercraft, you must obtain a Parks Canada AIS prevention inspection permit. For more
information and to book a watercraft inspection appointment

- [Water\\
Activity Rules and Regulations](https://parks.canada.ca/error404?item=web%3a%7b26DD5464-8A09-4F4F-BAAA-BA5DBD2B17D5%7d%40en)
- [Boating Safety Regulations](https://www.tc.gc.ca/eng/marinesafety/debs-obs-menu-1362.htm)

**Camping**

Camping is allowed only in designated campgrounds. The permit holder for the campsite is responsible for
the site, including cleanliness, noise levels and actions of site occupants. Parks Canada staff may
enter open and plainly visible areas of an unattended campsite to remove and secure plainly visible
wildlife attractants. Keep your campsite clean to reduce the risks to your personal safety and to
wildlife. Camping (including sleeping in a vehicle) is not allowed in roadside pullouts, parking lots,
trailheads and day-use areas. The maximum length of stay in an individual frontcountry campground is 14
nights. The maximum length of stay in an individual backcountry campground is 3 nights.

**Cannabis**

Cannabis, while legal, is strictly regulated in Canada. It is your responsibility to understand federal,
provincial, and municipal regulations for cannabis use.

- [Cannabis use at Banff National\\
Park](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/securite-safety/regles-rules/cannabis)

**Day-use**
**areas**

Day-use areas, such as picnic sites, are open from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. Fires are permitted only
where designated, metal fireplaces and cook shelters with stoves are provided by Parks Canada. Alcohol
use, smoking of any kind and camping are not allowed in kitchen shelters.

**Drones**

The recreational use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), also known as drones, is prohibited in all
national parks. Leave your drone at home or in your vehicle.

- [Use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles\\
(UAV) or drones in Canada’s national parks](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/permis-permit/drone)

**E-bikes**

E-bikes (pedal-electric power-assisted bicycles) are only permitted on roads and select trails in Banff
National Park. For more information on where pedal assist e-bikes are permitted, visit the [e-bike page](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/activ/cyclisme-biking/veloselectriques-ebikes).

**Entry**
**fees**

By law, all visitors to Banff National Park must purchase a national park entry pass. Passes are
available for purchase at park gates, visitor centres, campgrounds and at partner locations.

Parks Canada does routine checks asking visitors to show their entry pass. National park entry fees help
support visitor services and facilities within Banff National Park.

- [Park Passes Information](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/visit/passer-passes)

**Filming**
**and photography**

Temporary filming and photography activities have special considerations and a permit may be required.


- [Commercial film and photography\\
guidelines](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/permis-permit)

**Firearms and hunting**

The use of firearms (including pellet guns, bear bangers, bows, sling shots etc.) and hunting are not
permitted in Banff National Park. The possession of firearms is prohibited except at a person's primary
premises or in a motor vehicle that is travelling on a highway. During transport, firearms must be
unloaded and in a case or wrapped and tied securely in such a manner that no part of the firearm is
exposed.

- [National\\
Parks Wildlife Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-81-401/page-2.html#h-871108)

**Fires**

Fires are permitted only where designated fireplaces and cook stoves are provided by Parks Canada.
Random fires are not allowed.

Keep your campfire safe for you and the environment:

- **Keep fires small** – To reduce their impact on the environment, fires must be
contained within the designated metal fire boxes provided by Parks Canada and be kept to a
reasonable size.
- **Use firewood provided** – Transporting wood from elsewhere may spread invasive
insects and disease. Do not burn garbage or collect deadfall from the surrounding forest for
burning.
- **Never leave a fire unattended** – Fires must be attended at all times.
- **BBQs** – Charcoal barbeques are permitted. Discard cold ashes into a fire pit.
- **Extinguish fires completely** when not in use and before you depart.

**Fireworks**

Using, selling, or purchasing fireworks, or any other type of explosive, is not permitted.

**Fishing**

A national park fishing permit is required when angling in Canada's national parks. Provincial fishing
licenses are not valid.

The possession limit for all fish caught in Banff National Park is zero except for lake trout caught in
Lake Minnewanka reservoir for which the possession limit is two (2) fish.

Felt-soled boots are not permitted in any water body in Banff.

Anyone under the age of 16 may fish in the national parks without a permit if accompanied by a national
park fishing permit holder 16 years of age or older. However, their catch is then included within the
permit holder’s daily limit.

Consult these resources for the full regulations and more information:

- [Fishing Regulations Summary](https://parks.canada.ca/error404?item=web%3a%7bC86E3A09-0C45-4B88-938B-78E5E1022EA4%7d%40en)
- [National Parks\\
of Canada Fishing Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1120/index.html)
- [Open seasons, special\\
restrictions and closed water](https://www.pc.gc.ca/en/pn-np/ab/banff/activ/peche-fishing#Banff)

**Garbage**
**and litter**

Any unwanted food and garbage must be disposed of in wildlife-proof garbage bins, or secured in your
vehicle or food locker. Never leave garbage unattended. Do not store garbage in your tent, tent-trailer,
or open pick-up truck bed. Parks Canada staff are checking for unattended wildlife attractants.
Attractants found in plain view will be removed and safely secured to reduce the risks to you and to
wildlife. Never burn food or food related items, including bottles, cans and garbage. Leave no trace in
the backcountry. Pack out everything you pack in. Littering is a chargeable offence in Banff National
Park.

- [National Parks of\\
Canada Garbage Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-80-217/page-1.html)
- [Provincial Environmental Protection and\\
Enhancement Act](http://www.qp.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/E12.pdf)

**Generators**

To provide campers with a quiet and enjoyable experience within campgrounds, generator use is permitted
only between 9 a.m. and 10:30 a.m. and between 5 p.m. and 7 p.m. daily.

- [Important bulletins](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins)

**Motorized**
**vehicles**

Vehicles must remain on hardened surfaces, paved and gravel roads at all times. The use of Off-Highway
Vehicles (OHVs, e.g. ATVs, dirt bikes etc.) and Over-Snow Vehicles (OSVs, e.g. snowmobiles etc.) or any
other off-road vehicle is not allowed. Mopeds and scooters are motor vehicles and are not allowed on trails
or areas closed to motor vehicle traffic.

- [National Parks\\
Highway Traffic Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1126/FullText.html)

**Natural and**
**historic objects**

Leave what you find—it is the law. Natural and cultural resources such as rocks, fossils, artifacts, horns,
antlers, wildflowers and nests are protected and must be left undisturbed for others to discover and enjoy.


- [National Parks General\\
Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/N-14.01/page-2.html#h-360375)

**Parking**

Parking is permitted only in designated areas:

- [BanffNow](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/visit/banffnow) (up-to-date parking
information)
- [Town of Banff parking](http://banffparking.ca/)

**Permits and**
**business licenses**

All commercial activity is subject to [business licensing and permits](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/permis-permit).

**Pets**

You are responsible for your pets. Dogs cause stress for wildlife as they may be seen as a threat. Off-leash
dogs can trigger aggressive behaviour from wildlife. If you are planning to hike, camp or go into the
backcountry, consider leaving your dog at home.

To prevent unsafe situations for yourself, your dog, wildlife and other people:

- Dogs must be on a leash and under physical control at all times, it is the law. Never leave your pet
unattended in a vehicle, on your campsite, in your tent or anywhere else.
- Pets are not permitted in some areas. Look at the [important bulletins](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins) posted at
trailheads and at visitor centres to find out where restrictions are.
- Always clean up after your pet.

- [National Parks of\\
Canada Domestic Animals Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-177/FullText.html)

**Smoking and**
**vaping**

The Province of Alberta has established regulations that outline minimum distances from buildings, playgrounds
and other facilities with respect to cannabis and tobacco. These regulations are enforced in Banff National
Park. It is your responsibility to understand federal, provincial, and municipal regulations:

- [Tobacco and Smoking Reduction Act](http://www.qp.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/T03P8.pdf)
- [Cannabis use in the town of Banff](https://banff.ca/280/Cannabis-Use-in-Banff)
- [Alberta Cannabis legislation](https://www.alberta.ca/cannabis-framework.aspx#p6241s4)

**Speed**
**limits**

Obey speed limits at all times: 90 km/hr on major routes and 60 km/hr or less on secondary roads,
as posted.

- [National Parks\\
Highway Traffic Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._1126/FullText.html)

**Wildlife**

It is illegal to feed, entice or disturb any wildlife in a national park. Violators will be charged, subject
to a mandatory court appearance and could pay fines up to $25 000.

Be respectful when photographing wildlife. Never take selfies with wildlife. Animal behaviour is
unpredictable. Bring binoculars or a telephoto lens to capture wild animals in their natural environment.
When driving, obey all speed limits. If you see wildlife beside the road, slow down, stay in your vehicle,
take a photo and move on.

- [Learn more about respectful\\
behaviour around wildlife](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/visit/faune-wildlife)
- [National Parks Wildlife\\
Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/sor-81-401/page-1.html)
