# Welcome to Mt. Hood National Forest

The Mt. Hood National Forest encompasses 1.1 million acres. There are four ranger districts on the Forest that include a wide variety of ecosystems centered around Mt. Hood, the tallest mountain in Oregon: Barlow, Clackamas River, Hood River, and Zigzag Ranger Districts. The Forest has about 80 campgrounds, 20 sno-parks, and several commercial ski resorts including the iconic Timberline Lodge, a National Historic Landmark. Approximately one-third of the Forest is designated wilderness. The Forest also provides about one-third of the state's drinking and agricultural irrigation water.

## Plan Your Visit

![Split firewood](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/mthood/image/Cut%20firewood.jpg?h=eb250db1)

Personal use firewood permits are now available for the season on Mt. Hood National Forest through November 30.

All woodcutters must obtain a firewood permit to cut and haul firewood from the Forest. Permits are available at a district office or by submitting a [firewood permit application](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/159095).

[Firewood Permits](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mthood/permits/firewood)

![Road closed signs and jersey barriers at a bridge in the Forest.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/mthood/image/IMG_0479.jpeg?h=71976bb4)

With thousands of miles of Forest Service Roads across the Mt. Hood, conditions can vary and natural events can occur unexpectedly. Always plan ahead before visiting. Contact a local district office for details.

Some Forest Roads close seasonally. Refer to our free [Motor Vehicle Use Maps](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mthood/maps-guides/motor-vehicle-use-maps) for more information.

[National Forest Roads](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mthood/conditions/national-forest-roads)

![Man with ear protection holding a gun and shooting at a target in a gravel pit.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/mthood/image/target-shooting-gravel-pit.png?h=6afbeef0)

Target shooting is allowed on the national forest when done safely and with respect to the resources and public land. It is your responsibility to know all firearm regulations.

[Guns & Target Shooting](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/mthood/safety-ethics/firearms-target-shooting)

![Hand holding a cell phone in an aspen forest.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/mthood/image/phone%20hand%20app%20card%20flag.jpg?h=9437d2a8)

The National Forests and Grasslands app is currently available for iOS- and Android-powered devices. It can be downloaded for free through the App Store and Google Play. Download maps ahead of time, get safety alerts, plan your trip and find national forest sites near you.

[Download the App](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/maps/mobile-app)

![An image showing portions of three photos in a graphic: fire burning duff, trees in a treated stand and a new wooded trail bridge.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/image/Northwest%20Forest%20Plan.png?h=8b8b4309)

The Northwest Forest Plan covers 24.5 million acres of federally managed lands in California, Oregon, and Washington. It was established in 1994 to address threats to threatened and endangered species while also contributing to social and economic sustainability in the region. Over 30 years later, the Northwest Forest Plan needs to be updated to accommodate changed ecological and social conditions.

[NW Forest Plan](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/planning/northwest-forest-plan-amendment)
