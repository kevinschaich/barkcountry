# Holly Springs “Sinkhole” Mountain Bike Trail Reopens

**Release Date:** May 11, 2022

NEBO, NC (May 11, 2022) – The Holly Springs Trail, located in the Upper Creek area of Burke County on Pisgah National Forest’s Grandfather Ranger District, has reopened as of today to hikers and mountain bikers. The trail (TR #273), has been temporarily closed since February 2022 for closure of an abandoned mine shaft and trail maintenance.

Known locally as the “Sinkhole” trail, this popular mountain bike trail descends a ridge, passing a 30 foot deep, 6 foot wide abandoned mine shaft dubbed the “sinkhole”. Over the last decade there have been multiple minor incidents involving people and animals falling into the vertical hole. Due to these safety concerns, the US Forest Service determined the best course of action was to permanently mitigate the mine shaft. Several options were reviewed including closing or relocating the trail, filling the hole, or building a permanent fence. Ultimately the 12 foot by 10 foot flat grate covering the sinkhole was determined to be the best solution to meet the safety requirements of US Forest Service trails while maintaining the character of the rest of the Holly Springs Trail.

“Because of the remote location of the sinkhole and the importance of the trail to the local mountain bike community, we knew we would need to work with professional trail builders to maintain the backcountry character of the trail while safely installing the cover,” explains Lisa Jennings, Recreation Manager for the Grandfather District. “Black Diamond Trail Designs did a great job keeping what folks love about the trail. The result is a trail that is safer and more sustainable but still provides a great technical riding experience.”

Under the direction of the Grandfather District, and with support from volunteers of the Northwest NC Mountain Bike Alliance, professional trail builders with Black Diamond Trail Designs secured the sinkhole and maintained 1 mile of trail north of the sinkhole to Little Chestnut Road. The sinkhole is now covered with an aluminum grate that is safe to walk or ride over. Bike-optimized trail maintenance focused on hardening the trail with rockwork, installing drainage in steep sections, and generally minimizing impacts to the rooted, backcountry character that distinguished the Holly Springs experience for mountain bikers and hikers.

The Holly Springs “Sinkhole” Trail is rated as a most-difficult trail open to mountain biking and hiking only. For more information about mountain biking opportunities on the Grandfather Ranger District contact the District Office at 828-652-2144.
