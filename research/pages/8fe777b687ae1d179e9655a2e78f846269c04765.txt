## Welcome to Midewin National Tallgrass Prairie

(Pronounced “mid-DAY-win”)

The Midewin National Tallgrass Prairie was 
established in 1996 and is the first national 
tallgrass prairie in the country. It is 
administered by the US Forest Service, with the 
support of hundreds of volunteers and partner 
agencies, businesses, and organizations. Created 
from the former Joliet Army Ammunition 
Plant, Midewin remained largely closed to the 
public for many years while the

Army cleaned up contamination remaining from decades of TNT manufacturing and packaging.

Today, about 19,000 acres of Midewin are open to the public, including 30 miles of trails. 22 
miles of trail are for shared use by hikers, bicyclists, and equestrians. 8 miles of trail are for 
hiking only. Bicycles, horses, and leashed dogs are restricted to designated trails at all times. 
Midewin public lands are open from 4 am until 10 pm daily.

Picnic tables and portable toilets are available at all trailheads. There are currently no sources of 
water at Midewin other than at the Midewin Welcome Center. Bulletin boards located at all 
trailheads are regularly updated with important information. Please check for the latest news 
before heading out on the trail. Enjoy your visit.

### Midewin NTP Welcome Center

Open from 8 am until 4:30 pm Monday through Friday and on Saturdays in season. There are 
displays about the natural and cultural history of Midewin, an information counter for maps and 
questions, and a small bookshop.

### Dogs

Dogs are welcome at Midewin as long as they are kept on a maximum 6-foot leash and on a trail.

### Livestock

Cattle are helping maintain grassland bird habitat at Midewin. Their curiosity or maternal 
instincts may cause them to approach visitors. Please keep you distance from the cows and keep 
pasture gates closed.

### Hunting

Midewin is open for spring turkey hunting and for fall deer hunting under special restrictions. 
During firearm seasons, all non-hunting visitors to Midewin must stay on trails. Check the 
Midewin website for more information on hunting.

### Traffic

Heavy equipment uses the roads at Midewin for demolition, restoration, or farming. Operators 
may not be able to hear or see you. Stay away from operating equipment and watch for road 
traffic.

### Motor Vehicle Use

The map displays what types of motor vehicle are currently allowed and where. Only designated 
routes shown on the map are open to motorized use, and all motorized vehicles must be street 
legal under Illinois state law. Snow is not removed on these routes.

### In Case of Emergency

Call 911 for medical emergencies. Otherwise, contact the US Forest Service at (815) 423-6370.
