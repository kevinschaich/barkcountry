# OPERATING AND SAFETY PLAN CONDITIONS

## B. General Operating Conditions

1. Permittees are responsible for informing guests or clients of any potential hazards prior to starting a trip.

2. Any person(s) responsible for guiding trips and/or instructing guests or clients shall be familiar with the area, and 
be competent and experienced in conducting safe trips and activities.

3. Permittees and employees shall have a 10-unit first aid kit as a minimum for each 20 people present. The first aid 
kit shall available at campsites and on all trips. Outfitters and guides shall maintain First Aid/CPR training. Proof 
must be available during all operations.

4. All equipment shall be in good condition and fully functional.

5. Nonsystem trails will not be repeatedly used, cleared, or created.  Authorization to use or maintain existing 
nonsystem trails must be obtained from the appropriate District Ranger.

6. All gates encountered along trails will be closed unless otherwise posted.

7. Shortcutting trails and switchbacks are prohibited.

8. Leave No Trace principals will be incorporated into all operations. All outfitters and guides will be familiar with 
and will practice these outdoor ethics. If the use of off highway vehicles is authorized, all outfitters and guides 
will follow “Tread Lightly” principles. (Information on these operational techniques is available through the 
permitting Ranger Station.)

9. Violation(s) of New Mexico State game laws while operating under this permit **shall be cause for permit 
termination** for up to 3 years

10. Permittees and their employees shall familiarize themselves with the camping and smoking restrictions in effect, 
inform members of their party of such restrictions, and will be responsible for compliance with such restrictions 
while within the National Forest.

11. The Permitee is **required** to notify **both** permit administrators at the affected Ranger District **and** the 
administering District before each trip or activity. **This notification shall occur at least 48 hours prior to each 
trip.** Notification shall be made by mailing or faxing a trip plan. The outfitter/guide will keep a copy on hand for 
the duration of each trip. Multiple District trip plans are not preferred. When feasible, limit trip plans to a single 
District and include sufficient detail that District permit administrators can complete field inspections. See contact 
list above for district administrators.

## D. Livestock Operations

1. Grazing of livestock is not authorized except by prior written approval, decided on a case-by-case basis.  Requests 
to graze livestock must specify areas to be used.

2. Without prior approval, livestock will be highlined and fed processed certified weed free feed and/or certified 
weed free hay.

3. Livestock feeding is prohibited in the following areas:  within all wet meadows, cienegas, and "dry" lake beds; 
within all riparian areas (bottoms); within 300 yards of all springs, seeps, stock waters, and watershed structures 
(regardless of whether fenced or not).

4. Use of existing corrals is prohibited without prior written permission of the grazing permittee responsible for its 
maintenance (District contact persons can provide this information). Temporary electric fence corrals are 
authorized for control of livestock.  Such corrals will be located away from trails and water, and the electric fence 
and all hardware will be removed after each use.

5. No loose herding of pack stock shall be allowed on Forest development trails.

6. Manure piles shall be scattered.

7. Upon the death of any stock used in this operation within National Forest boundaries, the permittee shall locate 
the carcass at least 100 feet from water and a minimum of 300 feet from any trail.  Notification of the death will 
be made to the permit administering Ranger District.

8. Only riding or pack stock actually used for a particular operation shall be permitted.

9. Please slow down and use caution around livestock operations, especially while operating machinery 
around cattle being driven. Livestock in this area require several days gathering prior to moving and 
driving courteously will help the livestock permit holders in their effort to stay gathered.

10. The special use permit issued to the holder above authorizes approved activities on National Forest System 
Lands within the boundaries of the Lincoln National Forest. It is the outfitter’s responsibility to be aware of
the ownership status of land they are traveling through. It is the outfitter’s responsibility to determine 
whether or not the U.S. Forest Service has obtained a right-of-way for public use through the land managed 
by other federal agencies, state or private property.

## F. Operator Camps

1. Caches, permanent camps and/or improvements shall not be allowed in the White Mountain or Capitan 
Wilderness Areas without prior approval from the District Ranger.

2. Specific campsites will be identified and submitted with the trip itinerary.

3. Camps may be set up a maximum of five (5) days prior to use. A camp may remain in any one location up to 15 
days provided that guests are present. However, if such use is causing site degradation, it will be moved sooner; 
greater number of individuals/tents and the presence of stock will mean heavier impact and a shorter stay per 
camp site. Camps and equipment shall be removed within five (5) days after use unless approved in advance by 
the appropriate District Ranger.

4. The boughs or branches from live trees may not be utilized for camp or other use.  Use of dead native material for 
camp accessories such as hitch and game hanging rails and tent frames shall be permitted.  When camps are
dismantled, native materials shall be scattered or placed so they blend into the natural setting. Campsites will be 
restored to as natural a condition as possible.

5. All camps will be kept in a clean and orderly condition.

6. Temporary pit toilets are recommended at camps with five (5) or more people that are occupied longer than one 
day.  Toilets shall be located out of flood plains and at least 100 feet from live water.  Pits shall be of sufficient 
depth to permit covering within at least one foot of soil to ground level after use.

7. Camps shall be located a minimum of 100 feet from streams; 300 feet from springs; 300 yards from isolated water 
sources, and 100 feet from system trails where topography allows. Washing of equipment, dishes, clothing, etc. 
and bathing should be conducted at least 200 feet from any water source.

8. All refuse will be packed out. No trash or food waste will be buried or burned.

## G. General Wilderness/Wilderness Study Area Provisions:

1. Maximum group size in WildernessAreas shall be limited to 25 persons (inclusive of permittees, any employees 
of permittees, and guests or clients) and 15 head of pack and saddle stock (combined).

2. Pack it in, pack it out. Cat holes are the preferred method of human waste disposal. Please refer to the “Leave No 
Trace” guidelines for specific details of construction and general outdoor ethics.

3. Camp stoves are the preferred method for cooking. The amount of dead and down wood at the site, presence of a 
fire ring, fire danger and need for a campfire should be evaluated prior to starting a fire. Standing dead or green 
wood shall not be used for campfires. Fires should be built using a fire pan. Rock rings will not be constructed.

4. It is prohibited to use or possess motorized equipment or mechanized transport in designated Wilderness Areas. 
This includes but is not limited to motor vehicles, bicycles, chainsaws, generators, cordless electric drills, and 
game carts.

## G. Motorized Travel:

1. Motorized travel shall conform to the Lincoln National Forest Travel Management Rule. Free copies of the 
current Motor Vehicle Use Map can be obtained at any Lincoln National Forest Office.

2. Each vehicle shall have a first aid kit suitable to the vehicle’s capacity.

3. Maintain control of clients at all times when using off highway vehicles (OHVs) Be courteous to other recreation 
users by riding single file on the right side of the trail/route.

4. Clients should be instructed in the “Tread Lightly “techniques of OHV use, including reducing speed through 
muddy areas to minimize impacts to the road and to prevent rutting and deepening of mud holes and ruts.

5. Maintain slow speeds when there is minimal sight distance.

6. All non-motorized users, including users with livestock, have the right-of-way. If there is not enough 
room on the trail to get by, pull over and/or dismount your motorized equipment to allow other 
recreational users to get by.

7. OHV trips are not allowed until snow has melted from the roads and trails and they have had a chance to 
drain and dry out.

8. OHV personal protection gear needs to be worn at all times by the guide and the clients, including 
helmets, gloves, eyewear, and protective clothing.

## H. Other Conditions:

1. If an animal is killed and processed on National Forest lands, the following provisions apply:  the remains of 
processed animals may be left at the kill site.  Remains of processed animals will not remain at camp sites, but, 
will be removed and properly disposed of by the permittee.

2. Dogs used for hunting will be well trained. If wildlife are improperly chased, those dogs will be removed from 
service until properly trained.

3. When kept on National Forest Land overnight, dogs will be staked or kept in vehicles. Dogs will not be left 
unattended for any length of time on National Forest System Lands. All fecal matter should be scattered.
