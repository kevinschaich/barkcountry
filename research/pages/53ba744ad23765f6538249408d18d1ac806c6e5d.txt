# Pets

|     |
| --- |
| ![web_pets](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/images/web_pets.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)<br>Pets—dogs, cats, birds or any other animal—are wonderful! They provide comfort and companionship. You and your pets are welcome to visit Crater Lake National Park but there are a few rules and regulations that apply to visiting with your pet. <br>### Where Can I Walk With or Carry My Pet?<br>(See [Superintendent's Compendium](https://www.nps.gov/crla/learn/management/superintendent-s-compendium.htm) 36 CFR §2.15 – Pets) <br>Hikers may only be in possession of one leashed pet per person on trails. Pets must be leashed and in physical control of the hiker at all times. Pets may accompany you in the following areas:<br>1. Mazama Campground and all picnic areas<br>2. Along established roads and within 50 feet of the paved surfaces<br>3. On parking areas and paved walkways<br>4. Only on these designated trails:<br>**Lady of the Woods** (as long as the trail is free of significant snow)<br>**Godfrey Glen**(as long as the trail is free of significant snow) <br>**Annie Spur Trail (** connects PCT hikers to Mazama Village) <br>**Grayback Drive**(accessible when East Rim Drive is open)<br>**Pacific Crest Trail** (the official PCT, not the "alternate" PCT along the rim of the lake) <br>Trail descriptions are found on this website under [hiking trails](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/hiking.htm) and a map is available in the [park newspaper](https://www.nps.gov/crla/learn/news/newspaper.htm) or they can be found by zooming in on the digital [park map](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/maps.htm). Visitors can also use the "Find a Location" feature and enter the name of an individual trail.<br>**Whether they are on a leash, being carried, or in any sort of pack, pets are not permitted on the [Cleetwood Cove Trail](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/cleetwood-cove.htm).**<br>#### In summer and fall<br>Rim Village is a great place to walk your pet, with paved paths, roadways and a picnic area, where you can get uninterrupted views of the lake. Pets are also welcome to walk around the campground loops at Mazama Village and all [auto pullouts](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/overlooks.htm) around the park.<br>#### In winter and spring<br>Pets are welcome to walk along the parking lots in Rim Village or on top of the snowpack beside the roadway. Pets on leash and in physical control of their owner are allowed on paved roads throughout the park that are free of significant snow, in plowed parking lots, and up to 50 feet away from these paved, snow-free areas on the snowpack. <br>The Pacific Crest Trail is open year-round to pets, whether it is covered by snow or not.<br>### Prohibited Areas<br>\- Per the Superintendent’s Compendium: “Pets are prohibited from entering Crater Lake, all streams, and any body of water within the park.”<br>\- All hiking and designated ski trails/routes except for those listed above.<br>**36 CFR 2.15(a)(1)**<br>It is prohibited to “possess a pet in a public building...or any structure or area closed to the possession of pets by the superintendent.” This includes visitor centers, Crater Lake Lodge, all Mazama Village and Rim Village public buildings, <br>Service animals (any dog individually trained to do work or perform tasks for the benefit of an individual with a disability)when accompanying an individual with a disability, may go wherever visitors are allowed.<br>![A black Labrador on a leash sits in the snow next to a pair of gray snowhoes.](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/images/blk-lab-on-leash-PCT-sm_1.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)The Pacific Crest Trail (PCT) is open year-round to pets This Labrador is leashed and awaiting a winter hike. NPS Photo/Stephanie Duwe <br>### Why Should My Pet be on a Leash?<br>_All leashes must be a maximum of six feet per 36 CFR 2.15(a)(2)_<br>FOR THE SAFETY OF YOUR PET<br>Off-leash pets may easily be lost, injured, or even killed, especially near cliff edges.<br>FOR THE SAFETY OF ALL VISITORS<br>Some people fear pets, and normally well behaved pets can be stressed in unfamiliar surroundings, by crowds, and wildlife.<br>FOR THE PRESERVATION OF PLANTS Pets may dig or trample fragile vegetation.<br>FOR THE SAFETY OF WILDLIFE Off-leash pets can flush animals out of nesting sites or burrows, destroy wildlife homes, and leave behind a predator scent.<br>![A pet safety chart shows outside temperatures versus inside a car temperatures.](https://www.nps.gov/crla/planyourvisit/images/car_temp_guide.jpg?maxwidth=650&autorotate=false)<br>Temperatures inside vehicles rise quickly, and pets left in them can overheat and die. A cracked window provides little relief.<br>It is legal to leave your pet unattended in a vehicle. However, if the conditions create a danger to the animal, or if the animal becomes a nuisance, your pet can be seized by law enforcement and you may be cited.<br>Pets may not be left unattended and tied to vehicles, trees or other objects. |
