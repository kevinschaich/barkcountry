# Malheur National Forest invites community to participate in National Trails Day events

## Join the Forest Service on June 6 to celebrate trails and promote trail stewardship

**Release Date:** May 26, 2026

**Contact Information:** [SM.FS.malpubinfo@usda.gov](mailto:SM.FS.malpubinfo@usda.gov)

Malheur National Forest invites the public to participate in Trek through the Malheur ~ A Self-Guided Trail Challenge, as we celebrate National Trails Day in events designed to leave trails better than we find them.

National Trails Day was established by the American Hiking Society in 1993 and occurs annually on the first Saturday in June. It brings together federal land managers, partners, and volunteers to celebrate trails and accomplish trail stewardship projects to ensure they are safe, well-maintained, easy-to-follow and accessible.

Events on the Malheur National Forest found at [https://americanhiking.org/national-trails-day/find-an-event/](https://americanhiking.org/national-trails-day/find-an-event/)

- Trek through the Malheur ~ A Self-Guided Trail Challenge registration is open June 1, 2026, until October 31, 2026. You can fill out this [survey](https://forms.office.com/Pages/ResponsePage.aspx?id=5zZb7e4BvE6GfuA8-g1GlzKMQKQ80zhPnSF9YmraiyRUQUlXNlpaWEo3UVZDUzJSRzE0UE5JWlQzVC4u) to register your self-guided hike on the Malheur National Forest and provide feedback.

- Eastern Oregon Trail Alliance will host the Magone Trail Network Volunteer Weekend, at The Hub Trailhead June 6 & 7, 2026, from 9:00am to 3:00pm.

- Harney County Snowmobile Club Trail Clearing Day will be held July 4, 2026, from 10:00am to 5:00pm.

The Forest Service-managed trail system, at more than 165,000 miles, is the largest public trails system in the United States. In 2025, volunteers and partners accounted for more than 60% of all trail maintenance accomplishments (nearly 26,000 miles).

The agency is encouraging organizations to contact their local ranger district to get details about the Adopt-a-Trail program, which engages trained partners and volunteers who can independently steward National Forest System trails. Participants may maintain a specific trail or join a broader maintenance group. In some situations, organized trail partners may be well‑positioned to co-manage Adopt-a-Trail efforts in coordination with Forest Service staff.
