## Spotlights

![LRC Timed Reservations](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/deschutes/LRC%20Timed%20Reservations.png?h=2a479378)

Reserve your Lava River Cave Timed Reservation Ticket to secure your timeslot for parking at Lava River Cave ahead of arrival. Lava River Cave Timed Reservation Tickets improves visitors’ safety and experience by providing timeslots available for reservation in advance, including same-day arrivals (subject to availability).

[Reserve Your Ticket](https://www.recreation.gov/timed-entry/10089508?tab=info)

![South Sister](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/deschutes/South%20Sister.jpg?h=19ad3fdd)

The Mount Jefferson, Mount Washington and Three Sisters Wilderness areas are home to iconic mountain peaks, high alpine meadows and lakes, and old growth forests. In an effort to preserve the natural beauty and wilderness experience for you and future visitors and to better manage recreation-related impacts, these areas are managed with use limits, or quotas, to ensure the protection of these landscapes now and into the future.

[Get Your Permit](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/willamette/permits/central-cascades-wilderness-permits)

![Central OR Fire Info Card (updated)](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/legacy-media/deschutes/Central%20OR%20Fire%20Info%20Card%20updated.png?h=2a479378)

Visit Central Oregon Fire Info for information on prescribed fire, wildfire and smoke on the Deschutes & Ochoco National Forests, the Prineville BLM and the Oregon Department of Forestry. Text "COFIRE” to 888-777 to receive prescribed burning text alerts.

[Central Oregon Fire Info](https://centraloregonfire.org/)

![An image showing portions of three photos in a graphic: fire burning duff, trees in a treated stand and a new wooded trail bridge.](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/styles/thumbnail/public/r06/image/Northwest%20Forest%20Plan.png?h=8b8b4309)

The Northwest Forest Plan covers 24.5 million acres of federally managed lands in California, Oregon, and Washington. It was established in 1994 to address threats to threatened and endangered species while also contributing to social and economic sustainability in the region. **Over 30 years later**, the Northwest Forest Plan needs to be updated to accommodate changed ecological and social conditions.

[Learn More](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/planning/northwest-forest-plan-amendment)
