# Savannah River Site Forest Announces First‑Ever Forest Fellows

_Joe Orosz_,
_Region 8/Savannah River Site Forest_

_June 11, 2026_

The Savannah River Site Forest has selected Eileen Eck and Kyle Cox as its inaugural 2026–2027 Forest Fellows, marking the launch of a program designed to develop emerging U.S. Forest Service leaders on the Savannah River Site. The year‑long fellowship provides high‑performing employees with advanced leadership exposure, specialized mentorship, and opportunities to help shape future natural resource stewardship across the site.

Created to invest in exceptional talent, the Forest Fellows program connects participants directly with senior leaders across the Savannah River Site and the Forest Service, offering a rare inside look at the decision‑making that guides land management, operations, and long‑term planning.

“The Forest Fellows program represents our intentional commitment to developing employees who have already distinguished themselves and are ready for broader leadership,” said Forest Supervisor Nicholas Pardi. “Eileen and Kyle bring expertise, curiosity, and a deep dedication to public service. We are proud to support them as they take this next step and help guide the Savannah River Site Forest into the future.”

**A Year of High‑Level Engagement and Leadership Growth**

During their fellowship year, Eck and Cox will participate in a structured set of experiences designed to broaden their perspectives and strengthen their ability to support the Savannah River Site’s mission. They will work closely with senior leaders, gaining deeper insight into how natural resource management aligns with the site’s operational needs.

Each Fellow will receive one‑on‑one mentorship from Forest Service leaders. Eck will work with senior leadership on the Monongahela National Forest in West Virginia, while Cox will engage with leaders from the National Forests in North Carolina. These assignments will expose them to new approaches, proven practices, and the complexities of leading forest‑level programs across diverse landscapes.

During their fellowship, Eck and Cox will also complete a capstone project focused on improving forest operations on the Savannah River Site, bringing back lessons learned, identifying opportunities for innovation, and strengthening the forest’s stewardship practices. Their work will directly support the site’s mission while preparing them for broader leadership roles within the Forest Service.

The Forest Fellows program reflects the Savannah River Site Forest’s commitment to developing leaders who can meet future challenges with skill and integrity, ensuring the agency continues its strong record of stewardship and service on the Savannah River Site.

**Meet the 2026–2027 Forest Fellows**

2026 Forest Fellow Eileen Eck

_(SRNS)_

Eileen Eck, Silviculturist

Eck currently serves as the silviculturist on the Savannah River Site Forest. She earned her bachelor’s degree in forestry and natural resource management from Southern Illinois University Carbondale in 2012. Earlier in her career, Eck worked as a supervisory forester on the Forest Service’s Region 9 Timber Strike Team, coordinating with staff across 17 national forests in the Northeast and Midwest to implement sustainable forest management prescriptions.

She also worked as a forester for a private ecosystem services company and as a resource forester with the Missouri Department of Conservation, where she was selected as the 2019 Statewide Employee of the Year. Her experience spans active land management, cross‑disciplinary coordination, and long‑range planning. Raised in Chicago, she credits Scouting and trips to the Indiana Dunes and Wisconsin’s Northwoods for inspiring her interest in forest ecology and conservation.

“After completing the Forest Service’s National Advanced Silviculture Program in 2025, I am excited for this opportunity to approach leadership from a broader perspective and further develop my skills as a versatile and effective leader across disciplines,” Eck said.

2026-2027 Forest Fellow Kyle Cox

_(SRNS)_

Kyle Cox, Supervisory Wildlife Biologist

Cox currently serves as the supervisory wildlife biologist on the Savannah River Site Forest. He earned his bachelor’s degree in wildlife biology from Clemson University in 2013. Before returning to the site, he served as the wildlife program manager on the Oconee Ranger District, where he strengthened the red‑cockaded woodpecker program, led outreach efforts, and expanded stewardship partnerships.

Cox began his Forest Service career as a wildlife technician at the Savannah River Site and later held temporary and term positions in wildlife research and fire in South Carolina and Georgia. A native of Aiken, he brings local knowledge and a strong commitment to wildlife conservation to the inaugural cohort.

“I want to leave a positive legacy through my achievements in wildlife and forest management and through the relationships I build,” Cox said. “This fellowship represents a significant opportunity to help me reach my full potential and strengthen my ability to serve the forests and communities I care about.”

**A Foundation of Excellence**

Since 1951, the U.S. Forest Service has managed the natural resources of the Savannah River Site in partnership with the U.S. Department of Energy. Through sustainable forest management, research collaboration, and stewardship of wildlife and plant habitats, the Forest Service works to maintain healthy, resilient ecosystems across the site. This long‑standing partnership supports national priorities in conservation, land restoration, and responsible resource management while contributing to the broader mission of the Savannah River Site.
