Hiking at Chiricahua is an experience like no other. Many of the trails immediately bring visitors into an epic landscape of rocks of all shapes and sizes. Visitors don't just gaze at the formations from above, but stand under and beside the towering giants.

There are many hiking routes throughout the park. Elevations vary from 5,400 feet to 7,000 feet.

No

[Season\\
\\
Year Round](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hike-chiricahua.htm#InfoAccordian__Item__Description--7)

Hiking the Chiricahua Mountains can be amazing any time of year. Summer temperatures can often reach the 80s and 90s. Summer monsoons can provide cooler temperatures than the rest of Arizona. Winters can leave ice or snow on the trails, but daytime highs are often pleasant. Come prepared for quick shifts in weather.

Accessibility Information

Bonita Creek Trail is fully paved. Under normal conditions, it is flat and wide enough for mobility devices.

A portion of the Massai Point Trail is paved, but may not be accessible to non-motorized devices due to steep grade.

  - Type:Hiking

[![trees and grass in front of a mountain](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/0844CCFA-CC3B-F686-D98605D7A6F8EA5C.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/bonita-creek-loop.htm)

This Bonita Creek loops around the picnic area and takes you along the intermittent Bonita Creek. Watch for Apache plume and prickly poppy flowers in spring and early summer. Arizona whitetail deer are frequently seen in the lower portion of Bonita Canyon. This is a good trail to view grassland birds.

Winding along Bonita Creek, usually dry, this trail connects the Bonita Creek and Faraway Ranch Picnic Areas. Look for wild animals, including migrating birds, deer, coatimundi, and javelina. This is an easy 1/2 mile trail.

Beginning from the Visitor Center, Campground, or Faraway Ranch picnic area, this 1.2 mile/1.9 kilometer leisurely walk goes by the Faraway Ranch Historic District, Stafford Cabin, and Silver Spur Meadow. This is a dog-friendly trail.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Lower Rhyolite Canyon Trail**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/lower-rhyolite-canyon-trail.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Location:Visitor Center
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Cactus in front of large canyon](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/B18C1542-FC29-A30B-E4A1039E1677410B.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/lower-rhyolite-canyon-trail.htm)

A good way to view the rocks without driving up the road, this mostly-level trail takes you to Rhyolite Canyon, a canyon lined with rock pinnacles. Walk 1.5 miles / 2.4 km until you reach the junction between Upper Rhyolite Canyon and Sarah Deming trails. Return the same way.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Trail to Natural Bridge**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/natural-bridge-trail-chiricahua-nm.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:3-4 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Location:Trailhead for Natural Bridge Trail
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Rock formations with a natural bridge featured.](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/6A95D12A-C482-991C-A2F578CE05D855F8.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/natural-bridge-trail-chiricahua-nm.htm)

This trail is a great opportunity to avoid the crowds in March and to see wildlife. It crosses through a stunning variety of ecosystems before ending at a bridge overlook. The small water-carved bridge is located across the canyon. This hike is about the journey, not the bridge. Return the same way.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Echo Canyon to Visitor Center**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/echo-canyon-to-visitor-center.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:2-4 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Location:Echo Canyon Trailhead
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Hillside full of rock formations](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/A6356DA8-F94B-0B6A-F748721806604CC9.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/echo-canyon-to-visitor-center.htm)

This moderate, 4.2 mile/6.8 kilometer trail begins at Echo Canyon trailhead and consists of Echo Canyon, Upper Rhyolite Canyon, and Lower Rhyolite Canyon trails ending at the Visitor Center. It goes downhill through spectacular rock formations in Echo Canyon. The last half of the hike is in shaded oak woodlands. Make sure you have transportation back to your vehicle or ride the seasonal hikers' shuttle at 9:00 am.

The most impressive moderate hike in the park. Hike 3.3 miles through balancing rocks, the unique tunnel-like grottoes, narrow slots, and below cliffs of towering pinnacles. No pets.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Sugarloaf Mountain Trail**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/sugarloaf-mountain-trail.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:1-2 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Location:Sugarloaf Mountain Trailhead
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Green hillside with mountains and rock formations in the distance](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/A5E5DBB7-F313-EE45-83B137C8C4039E81.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/sugarloaf-mountain-trail.htm)

This moderate trail to the top of Sugarloaf Mountain is 1.8 miles/2.8 kilometers roundtrip. Rising above the surrounding canyons, the summit of Sugarloaf Mountain at 7,310ft/2,228m is one of the highest points in the monument. The dark rock is dacite, evidence of a lava flow. Lightning activity is monitored from the Civilian Conservation Corps constructed fire lookout during the summer. Return the same way. Access is from the Sugarloaf parking lot.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Inspiration Point Trail**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/inspiration-point-trail.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:3-4 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Location:Echo Canyon or Massai Point Trailheads
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Green valley in distance lined with rock pinnacles](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/A50E6780-F497-C05A-34B03B33FAFBFDCE.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/inspiration-point-trail.htm)

This 5.4 mile / 8.6 kilometer strenuous trail takes you to the Inspiration Point overlook. The route consists of Ed Riggs, Mushroom Rock, and Inspiration Point trails. Excellent views of Cochise Head and Rhyolite Canyon make this a great rest stop!

- Chiricahua National Monument [**Heart of Rocks Loop**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/heart-of-rocks-loop.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:4-6 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Balanced rock formation](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/A486A890-07E5-B52A-00306CFBC76053A7.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/heart-of-rocks-loop.htm)

The Heart of Rocks Loop is a 7.3 mile/11.8 kilometer roundtrip hike to many of the most unusual rock formations in the monument, including Pinnacle Balanced Rock and Big Balanced Rock. Start the loop to the left and hike clockwise for the best views and easiest walking. Lots of rock steps make this a challenging loop, but it's worth the effort. There are three different ways to get to the loop, all of them being the same distance.

- Chiricahua National Monument [**The Big Loop**](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/the-big-loop.htm)







  - Type:Hiking
  - Locations:Chiricahua National Monument
  - Duration:4-7 Hours
  - Reservations:No
  - Pets:No
  - Season:Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall
  - Time Of Day:Day, Night, Dawn, Dusk

[![Mountain with several rock formations](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/cropped_image/secondary/9A88A2D6-91E3-E555-2AEE6F2F40F75B0A.jpg?maxwidth=200&quality=90)](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/the-big-loop.htm)

For folks wanting to do everything, this 9.5 mile route consists of the Echo Canyon, Upper Rhyolite Canyon, Sarah Deming, Heart of Rocks, Big Balanced Rock, Inspiration Point, Mushroom Rock, and Ed Riggs trails. It features our balanced rocks. The trail begins from the Echo Canyon trailhead.
